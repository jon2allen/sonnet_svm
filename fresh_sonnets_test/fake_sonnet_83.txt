**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
And bear’st the bloom from beauty’s blushing cheek,
Whilst golden locks to silver threads are fled,
And love’s sweet fire doth cold and pale grow meek.

The rose, once proud in summer’s wanton prime,
Now droops her head beneath the autumn’s breath,
And time, that carves his name in every clime,
Doth write his lines where youth once laughed at death.

Yet mark thee well, though seasons rise and fall,
And mortal things must fade like morning dew,
The earth still sings her everlasting call,
And spring returns in robes of emerald hue.

So take this truth, though time may steal thy grace—
The stars abide, and love outlives the race.
