Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours in silent haste do glide,
The rose, once flushed with youth’s unblushing pride,
Now droops her head where time hath laid to sleep.
O fleeting bloom! O transience most deep,
That steals the hue from cheeks where love abide,
And turns to dust the joys we fain would hide,
Yet leaves the thorn to prick the hearts that weep.

The brook that laughed in silver, bright, and clear,
Now murmurs low of days it cannot stay,
While willows weep in sorrow, dark and drear,
For summer’s light that fades to winter’s gray.
Yet in this change, a truth doth still appear—
The soul outlives the hand that bids decay.
