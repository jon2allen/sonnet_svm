**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on silver streams,
Whose laughter wakes the violet’s tender head,
Why steal ye thus, like thieves of midnight dreams,
And leave the earth in ashen silence spread?

The rose, once proud in crimson robes arrayed,
Now bows her brow to time’s ungentle hand,
And all the sweets that summer’s breath conveyed
Lie withered where no springtide may command.

Yet mark how dawn, with fingers dipped in light,
Still paints the sky in hues of hope’s own dye,
And though the stars must fade before the night,
They kindle still the dark with borrowed eye.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet beauty lives in what it burns away.
