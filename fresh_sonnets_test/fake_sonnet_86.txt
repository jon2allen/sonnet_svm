**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal
The bloom from cheeks where roses once did dwell,
And bid the brook, that murmured soft and well,
Now hush its song beneath the winter’s chill.
The lark, that sang so sweet on yonder hill,
Now folds her wings where frost hath bound the fell,
And time, that carves his name where all things swell,
Doth mar the grace he erst did sweetly fill.

Yet mark how dawn, in robes of crimson dyed,
Doth kiss the earth wherewith her tears are wet,
And spring, though fled, shall rise in pride anew.
So fades the rose, yet in the bud doth hide
A promise fair as any sun hath set—
For beauty dies not, though its form we rue.
