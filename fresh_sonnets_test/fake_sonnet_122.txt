**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence glide,
And steal the rose from summer’s blushing cheek,
Whilst time, that thief, doth bid the bloom to seek
The barren earth where all fair things abide.
The brook, once bright with silver’s liquid pride,
Now murmurs low where winter’s fingers tweak
The withered reeds, and frost doth sharply shriek
O’er fields that once in emerald raiment sighed.

Yet lo! The lark still trills his matin lay,
And dawn doth kiss the hawthorn’s frost-kissed spray,
As though the world might pause to mark the day.
But ah! The sun, too swift, will haste away,
And leave thee, love, to count the hours’ decay—
Till time shall turn thy gold to ashen gray.
