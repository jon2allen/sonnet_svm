**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
And bear’st the bloom from roses ere they fade,
What art thou but a thief in twilight’s shade,
Who robs the earth of gold the sun hath spread?

The brook that laughed in silver, bright and clear,
Now whispers low beneath the withered bough,
While time, that mocks the heart with hollow vow,
Doth turn to dust the joys we held most dear.

Yet mark how nature, in her cunning guise,
Doth paint the woods with hues of crimson flame,
As though to cheat the eye with borrowed guise,
And bid the world forget her fleeting frame.

So beauty fades, and love must yield to years—
Yet in thy theft, O Time, the soul endears.
