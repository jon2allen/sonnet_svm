**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
And bear’st the bloom from beauty’s blushing cheek,
Whilst time, that tyrant, doth his scythe unsheathe,
And mows the meadows where the wildflowers spread.

The rose, once proud, now withers where it grew,
Its crimson hue to pale and ashen turned;
The brook, that sang so sweet in days of yore,
Now murmurs low where once its joy was spurned.

Yet mark thee well, though all must fade and fall,
The earth renews her robe in verdant grace,
And spring, with gentle hand, doth still recall
The lost delight to time’s unyielding face.

So too shall beauty, though it fade from sight,
Live on in verse, where time itself takes flight.
