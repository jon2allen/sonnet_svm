When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn yields to the evening’s dew.

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low of seasons passed away,
While trees that wore the summer’s canopy
Stand bare beneath the pale and wintry ray.

Yet in this hush, where beauty seems undone,
A seed takes root where none had deemed it so—
The earth, though stripped, shall bear the coming sun,
And time, though stern, doth bid the blossoms blow.

So take this truth, though all must fade and flee:
What dies in form lives on in mystery.
