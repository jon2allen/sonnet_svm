**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence glide,
And steal the rose from summer’s blushing cheek,
Whilst time, that thief, doth mock the hand we seek,
And turns to dust the joys we fain would hide.
The brook, once bright with laughter, now runs wide,
Its silver tongue grown hoarse with age’s shriek;
The oak, that stood so proud on yonder peak,
Bows low beneath the storm it once defied.

Yet in thy face, fair youth, the spring doth live,
Though winter’s breath hath kissed the fading leaf;
For beauty, like the dawn, doth still revive,
And time, though cruel, grants thee brief reprieve.
So pluck the bloom ere night its shade shall weave—
For all must fade, save love in memory’s sieve.
