Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson hue doth fade,
As whispered winds through silvered branches spread,
And kiss the earth where youthful petals laid.

O fleeting grace! Like morn’s first golden thread,
That weaves the sky ere noon’s bold light is made,
Thy beauty wanes, yet in my heart is read
The ghost of thee, in twilight’s pallid shade.

The brook that laughs, the oak that stands so tall,
Do mark the hours with slow and steadfast art,
While I, who loved thee, watch thee fade from all—

Yet in the hush of evening’s dying part,
Thy voice still lingers, soft as autumn’s call,
And time, that steals, shall not unmake thy heart.
