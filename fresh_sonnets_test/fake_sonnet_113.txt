**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, why dost thou steal so fast,
And leave behind thy traces, pale and thin?
The rose that blooms today in crimson cast
Tomorrow fades, its glory worn within.
The brook that laughs in silver, clear, and bright,
Shall bear no mark of joy when winter’s near;
The lark that soars at dawn’s first tender light
Shall silent fall when dusk draws dark and drear.

Yet in this flight, where time doth all consume,
There lies a grace that mocks the fleeting day—
The violet’s breath, the dew’s ephemeral bloom,
The fleeting glance that turns our night to grey.
So take, fair hour, what thou must take away,
But leave thy shadow where my heart may stay.
