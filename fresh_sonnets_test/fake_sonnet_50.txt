Upon the breath of fleeting hours we tread,
Where golden dawns in crimson robes do fade,
And beauty, like the rose in summer spread,
Doth bow her head where time hath laid his blade.
The brook that sings through meadows green and wide,
With silver tongue doth whisper secrets old,
Of love that blooms, then withers in the glade,
And tales of joy in twilight’s fold enrolled.

O cruelest thief! That steal’st the rose’s hue,
And dimm’st the star that lights the lover’s way,
Yet in thy wake, the earth doth still renew,
And spring’s sweet hand doth bid the winter stay.
So take my youth, but leave this truth to me:
That time may fade, yet love outlives thee.
