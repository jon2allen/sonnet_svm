**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting shade of gold that gilds the bough,
Where once the rose did blush in crimson pride,
Now winter’s breath doth whisper, soft and low,
And steals the bloom that summer’s hand supplied.
The brook that sang in silver, clear and bright,
Now murmurs ‘neath the ice’s frozen chain,
While time, that thief of all we hold most dear,
Doth turn to dust the joys we would retain.

Yet mark how yonder oak, though stripped and bare,
Still lifts its arms as though to greet the sky,
And in its boughs the robin, unafraid,
Sings of the spring that soon shall bid him fly.
So too the soul, though beauty fades away,
May find in time’s own course a sweeter lay.
