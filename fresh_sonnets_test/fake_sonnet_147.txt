**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal
The bloom from cheeks where roses once did dwell,
And steal’st the song from lips that time would quell,
Yet leave’st the echo of their sweet appeal.
The brook, that laughed in silver-edged delight,
Now whispers low beneath the withered bough,
Where once the lark did pierce the vaulted blue,
And dawn’s first blush made all the world seem bright.

Lo, where the violet bends her drooping head,
And autumn’s breath hath kissed the summer’s pride,
The fleeting joys that in thy bosom hide
Are but the ghosts of pleasures long since fled.
Yet in thy fall, O year, thou dost impart
A beauty deeper than the rose’s art.
