Upon the breath of fleeting hours we tread,
Where golden dawns in crimson twilight fade,
And beauty, like the rose in summer’s shade,
Doth bow her head where time hath softly spread.

The brook that sings through meadows green and wide,
Now murmurs low where winter’s frost hath lain,
Yet still the stars in heaven’s vault remain,
Though earth must yield to time’s unyielding tide.

O fleeting bloom! O transient, sweet delight!
Thou dost but mock the heart that clings to thee,
For time, that spares nor king nor flower bright,
Shall steal thy hue and leave but memory.

Yet in the hush of evening’s gentle fall,
Nature doth whisper—time consumes us all.
