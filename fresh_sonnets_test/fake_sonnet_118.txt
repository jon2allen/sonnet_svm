When golden hours like petals drift away,
And time’s unkind hand plucks the rose’s hue,
What once was bright must fade in dull decay,
As fleeting spring yields to the winter’s dew.
The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now whispers low of joys it once did know,
While youth, that danced so light on bended knee,
Creaks with the weight of years that come and go.

Yet in the hush where twilight weaves her spell,
A breath of bloom still lingers on the air,
As if some ghost of beauty dared to dwell,
And mock the grave with fragrance light and fair.
So take this hour, ere time shall steal its grace—
The rose, the brook, the light upon thy face.
