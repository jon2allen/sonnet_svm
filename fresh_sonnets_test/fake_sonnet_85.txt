Upon the Breath of Fleeting Hours We Tread

When golden dawn doth kiss the waking earth,
And dew-kissed blooms in trembling light unfold,
Time steals the blush of youth, the rose’s worth,
And leaves but thorns where petals once did hold.

O cruelest thief! That plucks the fairest hue
From heaven’s own cheek, and dims the starry eye,
Yet leaves the brook to murmur ever true,
And teaches winds to sigh, yet never die.

The oak, that stands where once the sapling played,
Now grips the sky with branches broad and wise,
While I, who walked where spring’s first footsteps strayed,
Find winter’s frost has silvered my disguise.

Yet in thy face, dear love, the seasons blend—
Time’s thief may steal, but beauty hath no end.
