**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on meadows bright,
Where blossoms blush and silver streams do play,
How swift ye steal the sun’s unyielding light,
And bear the rose’s glory swift away.

The lark that soars at dawn’s first trembling hue,
Now folds her wings as twilight dims the sky,
And time, that thief who spares nor old nor new,
Doth mark each joy with shadows drawing nigh.

Yet in thy face, fair child of earth and air,
The spring’s own grace doth linger, though it fade;
For beauty, like the dew at morning’s prayer,
Is but a loan the fleeting years have made.

So take this hour, ere time its debt reclaim—
Love’s bloom must wither, though its root be flame.
