Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose with hue of crimson bright,
Yet lo! the wind doth whisper soft, and spread
Its petals thin, as fleeting as the light.
The golden sun, that kissed the meadow’s face,
Now sinks beneath the hill in purple shade,
And leaves the earth in twilight’s cool embrace,
Where once the lark in joyous notes was laid.

O fleeting grace! O beauty’s fragile thread,
That weaves the spring in colors bold and fair,
Yet yields to autumn’s touch, and bows its head,
As silver frost doth claim the dying air.
So too shall pass the love that now doth burn,
As stars must fade when dawn begins to turn.
