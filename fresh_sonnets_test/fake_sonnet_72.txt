**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal
The bloom of rose and lark’s ascending cry,
Why dost thou mock the heart that longs to feel
Thy fleeting touch ere time denies the sky?

The brook, once bright with laughter, now runs low,
Its silver tongue grown weary of the song;
The oak, that stood so proud in summer’s glow,
Bends ‘neath the weight of winter’s frosty throng.

Yet in thy haste, thou leav’st a trace behind—
A petal’s blush, a leaf’s last trembling sigh,
As if to say, though fleeting be the mind,
Beauty endures where memory will not die.

So take my hand, swift hour, and lead me where
The earth still dreams, though time forgets to care.
