Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours in silent haste do glide,
The rose, once bold in crimson pride so deep,
Now scatters petals on the wind’s cold tide.
O fleeting bloom! O moment’s brief delight,
That mocks the hand which strives to hold thee fast,
As morning’s blush must yield unto the night,
And summer’s song in autumn’s sigh is past.

The brook that laughed in silver, bright, and free,
Now murmurs low ‘neath winter’s frozen breath,
Yet in its voice doth whisper unto me
Of time’s unyielding, ever-turning wheel of death.
So take this hour, though doomed to fade from sight,
For beauty lives but in the grasp of light.
