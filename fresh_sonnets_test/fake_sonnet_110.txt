**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting guest that through the meadows glide,
Where golden blooms in wanton clusters rise,
And silver streams with whispered lullabies
Do kiss the banks where drowsy willows bide—

Thou steal’st the rose from youth’s unheeding hand,
And with thy breath dost bid the violet fade,
While time, that thief, doth mark the sun’s parade,
And carves his name on every leaf’s command.

Yet in thy haste, what beauty dost thou leave!
The frost’s bright lace on winter’s hollow cheek,
The lark’s last song ere twilight’s shadows seek
The earth that once with summer’s blush did heave.

So take my days, but leave this truth to me:
That love outlives thee, though all else may flee.
