Upon the brow of Time, where seasons play,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson lips do part
To whisper secrets to the list’ning heart,
Yet lo! The wind doth steal its hues away.
O fleeting grace! O morn that fades to grey,
Where golden beams in silver streams depart,
And youth, once bright, now yields to age’s art,
As rivers carve the stone with ceaseless sway.

The lark, that soars where dawn and dusk entwine,
Sings not of joy, but of the hour’s deceit,
For beauty, like the dew on meadow’s line,
Melts where the sun doth press his burning feet.
Yet in the shade where memory doth pine,
Love’s ghost still walks where time and nature meet.
