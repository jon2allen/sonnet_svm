**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
And bear’st the bloom from yonder rose’s cheek,
Whilst golden suns in sapphire heavens spread,
Yet leave the earth in twilight’s chill to seek.

The brook that laughed in silver, bright, and clear,
Now murmurs low beneath the frost’s cold hand,
And summer’s song, so sweet, we held so dear,
Is lost where winter’s desolate wastes expand.

Yet mark thee well—though time doth fade the hue
Of petals kissed by dawn’s first tender light,
And youth’s fair face must yield to age’s rue,
Still nature’s heart beats ever in the night.

For though the rose must wither, fall, and die,
Her seed shall wake the spring in endless sky.
