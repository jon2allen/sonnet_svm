**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting shade of gold where dawns unfold,
Thy tender bloom doth blush but to decay,
As silver streams that time’s unkind hand hold
Do murmur soft their sorrows to the clay.
The rose, once proud in crimson robes arrayed,
Now scatters petals on the wanton air,
And summer’s song, in hollow winds betrayed,
Fades like the echo of a lover’s prayer.

Yet mark how winter, stern in frost’s embrace,
Doth cloak the earth in silence, stark and white,
Till spring, with fingers light as love’s own grace,
Unfurls the green from slumber’s long night.
So turns the wheel, and beauty, doomed to fade,
Lives but in verse where time may not invade.
