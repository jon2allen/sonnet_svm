**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the mead with gold of transient light,
Why dost thou mock the bloom that fades from view,
And leave the earth to wither in thy flight?

The lark that soars at dawn in joyous cry
Shall sing no more when dusk hath dimmed the sky.
The brook that laughs in silver, swift, and free,
Shall freeze to glass ere winter’s breath be nigh.

Yet in thy haste, thou dost not all destroy—
The seed that sleeps shall wake in vernal air,
And beauty, though it fade, shall rise anew,
A ghost of gold still trembling here and there.

So take my youth, but leave the hope I keep:
That time may steal, yet never quite shall reap.
