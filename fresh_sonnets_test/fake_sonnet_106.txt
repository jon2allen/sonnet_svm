**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting breath of time, that steals so sly,
The rose’s blush ere noon hath kissed the dew,
And turns the gold of summer’s vibrant hue
To ashen grey where once the lark did fly.
The brook, that sang so sweet in days gone by,
Now whispers low of joys it never knew,
While youth, once bright as dawn’s unbroken view,
Fades soft as stars when morning draws too nigh.

Yet mark thee well, though beauty’s bloom must fade,
And time’s harsh hand doth mar what once was fair,
The earth still keeps the seeds the seasons made—
A promise sown in silence, deep and rare.
For though the petal falls, the root remains,
And spring shall wake what winter’s chill restrains.
