Upon the Fleeting Breath of Golden Hours

O fleeting breath of golden-tinted days,
Where time doth steal the rose from beauty’s cheek,
And silver threads in nature’s tresses play,
As autumn’s sigh doth bid the summer seek.

The brook, once bright with laughter’s liquid song,
Now whispers low to moss-clad, weeping stone,
While zephyrs dance, yet dance not half so long
As when the earth was young, and love full-grown.

The lark, that scaled the heavens’ vaulted height,
Now folds her wing ‘neath twilight’s pallid hand,
And all the stars, once bold in endless light,
Dim to the dawn’s indifferent, shifting sand.

Yet in this hush, where time and beauty wane,
Love’s echo lingers—soft, yet not in vain.
