Upon the brow of Time, where seasons play,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson hue doth fade,
As fleeting as the dawn’s first golden blade,
Or silver streams that dance in sunlit spray.
O cruelest thief! Who steals the hours away,
And leaves but dust where once sweet graces swayed,
Yet in the hush of twilight’s soft parade,
The earth still sings what time cannot betray.

For though the petals fall, and youth decays,
And beauty’s fire must yield to winter’s chill,
The root runs deep where ancient wisdom stays—
A voice that murmurs from the verdant hill.
So let the wind bear what the heart still knows:
Love is the bloom, and time but thorn that grows.
