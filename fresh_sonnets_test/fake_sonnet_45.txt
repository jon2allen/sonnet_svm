**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
And bear’st the bloom from roses ere they fade,
Whilst golden suns in heaven’s vault are spread,
And time doth weave the seasons’ bright parade.

The lily droops, the violet bends low,
The brook that laughed now whispers soft and slow,
And summer’s song, once loud in joy’s glad show,
Now sighs beneath the autumn’s weight of snow.

Yet in thy haste, thou cruel thief of days,
Thou leav’st behind the echo of thy flight—
The star that gleams through twilight’s fading haze,
The moon that paints the hush of silver night.

So take my youth, but leave this truth to me:
Beauty must fade, yet love outlives thee.
