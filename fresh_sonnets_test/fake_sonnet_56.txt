Upon the brow of Time, where seasons play,
There blooms a rose whose crimson lips do fade,
As morning’s blush gives way to evening’s shade,
And beauty’s reign must yield to decay’s sway.
The lark that greets the dawn with song so gay,
Now hush’d, lies still where twilight’s shadows wade,
For fleeting hours no golden bond have made,
But steal the gold they lend, and slip away.

O cruelest thief! That dost in silence creep,
And rob the earth of all her sweetest store—
The violet’s breath, the brook’s unwearied sweep,
The oak’s proud strength that time shall bend no more.
Yet in thy theft, one truth doth stand most deep:
Love’s fire outburns the embers of thy lore.
