Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours in fleeting haste do glide,
The rose, once fair, must bow her head and weep,
As time’s unkind scythe lays her pride aside.
O cruelest thief! That steal’st the bloom of May,
And leaves but dust where once sweet nectar ran,
The brook that sang in silvered light all day
Now whispers low to some forgotten man.

Yet mark how nature, in her endless art,
Doth weave anew what time hath torn apart—
The bud breaks forth where frost had sealed the heart,
And dawn reclaims the night’s unyielding dark.
So too shall beauty, though it fade from sight,
Live on in verse beyond time’s mortal blight.
