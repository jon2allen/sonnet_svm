Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson lips do part
With whispers soft as twilight’s fleeting art,
Yet fades ere dawn may kiss the dews it shed.
O fleeting grace! O beauty swiftly sped,
Like silver streams that slip through mortal heart,
Or golden hours that, ere their tale impart,
Dissolve in mist where no man’s foot hath led.

The lark, that greets the morn with song so clear,
Must yield to night’s slow-creeping, silent hand;
The oak, that stood where tempests raged in fear,
Now bows his head to winter’s stern command.
Yet in the hush, where Time his scythe doth wield,
Love’s ghost still walks the fields of memory’s field.
