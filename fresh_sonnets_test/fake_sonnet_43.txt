**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
And bear’st the bloom from beauty’s cheek away,
While golden locks to silver threads decay,
And time’s harsh scythe lays low the summer’s head.

The rose, once proud in crimson robes arrayed,
Now droops her head where once she stood so red,
And winds that kissed her now her petals shred,
As youth and joy in dust are coldly laid.

Yet mark the brook that laughs through mossy stone,
Still dancing on though seasons rise and fall,
And stars that keep their ancient course alone.

So too my love, though time shall mar us all,
Shall live in verse when death hath claimed his own—
A light no winter’s breath may e’er enthrall.
