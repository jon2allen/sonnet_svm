**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence glide,
Like whispered verse upon the breath of May,
Thy fleeting grace doth bid the world obey,
Yet steals the rose ere noon hath turned to tide.
The brook, once bright with heaven’s reflected pride,
Now murmurs low where shadows softly play,
And time, that tyrant, wears the bloom away,
As love and leaf must yield to winter’s stride.

What hand may stay the swift and subtle hand
That paints the dawn, yet blots it with the night?
The lark ascends, the violet fades to sand,
And beauty, like the dew, takes sudden flight.
Yet in this loss, a truth doth firm remain—
That time, though cruel, makes all things fair again.
