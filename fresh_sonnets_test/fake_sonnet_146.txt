Upon the Breath of Fleeting Hours We Tread

O fleeting breath of hours that softly glide,
Like silver streams through meadows kissed by dawn,
Where golden blooms in transient splendor ride,
And beauty fades ere twilight’s veil is drawn.
The rose, once proud in crimson robes arrayed,
Now scatters petals to the wanton wind,
As time, that thief, doth steal the hues we made,
And leaves but dust where love and joy entwined.

Yet mark thee well, though seasons rise and fall,
And youth’s bright fire must yield to winter’s chill,
The earth, in slumber deep, shall wake anew,
And spring’s sweet song shall time’s harsh law fulfill.
For though all things must fade and fade they must,
In nature’s hand, their dust is ever just.
