When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn yields to the evening’s dew.

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low where shadows deepen fast,
And youth, that danced with careless revelry,
Must bow at last to age’s chilling blast.

Yet in the hush where twilight lingers long,
A whispered song the dying light bestows—
The lark’s last note, the nightingale’s sweet throng,
The sighing wind through autumn’s withered rows.

So beauty fades, and time will not delay,
But love outlives the swiftest hours’ decay.
