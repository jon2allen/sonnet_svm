When golden hours like petals drift away,
And time’s relentless hand doth mark the sand,
The rose, once bright in morning’s tender ray,
Now bows its head to autumn’s chilling hand.

O fleeting bloom! O beauty’s brief delight!
The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now whispers low beneath the fading light,
A dirge for joys that used to dance with glee.

Yet in the hush where twilight weaves her spell,
The stars arise to sing of constancy,
And though the earth her transient glories tell,
Love’s fire outburns time’s cold decree.

So take this hour, as seasons rise and fall,
For time may steal the rose, but not its thrall.
