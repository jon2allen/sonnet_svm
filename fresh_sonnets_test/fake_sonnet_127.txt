Upon the brow of time where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose whose crimson lips do fade,
As morning’s gold doth yield to evening’s shade,
And beauty’s fleeting grace by hours is led.

The silver streams that through the meadows glide,
Once mirrored skies in azure, bright, and wide,
Now whisper low of joys they could not bide,
For time, relentless, turns their pride to tide.

O fleeting sprite! Why dost thou mock mine eye
With blossoms fair that wither ere they’re known?
The lark’s sweet song, the violet’s tender sigh,
Are but the ghosts of pleasures overthrown.

Yet in this change, a truth doth still abide—
Love’s fire outlasts the embers time hath tried.
