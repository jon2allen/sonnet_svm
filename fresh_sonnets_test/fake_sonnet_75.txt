**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in splendour shine,
When dawn’s first blush doth kiss the waking earth,
And silver streams in whispered hymns combine,
To sing of love and time’s unmeasured worth.
Yet lo! the rose, so fair in morning’s light,
Doth fade ere noon, its petals swept away,
As fleeting as the stars that gleam at night,
Or dreams that vanish with the break of day.

The lark, that soars on pinions bold and free,
Must yield at last to winter’s cruel embrace,
And all the joys that dance so merrily
Are but the shadows of a fleeting grace.
Yet in this change, one truth doth still remain—
Beauty, though brief, shall live in verse’s reign.
