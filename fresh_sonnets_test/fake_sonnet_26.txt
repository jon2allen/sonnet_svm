Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson hue doth fade,
As fleeting as the dew on morning’s blade,
Or stars that in the twilight’s lap are shed.
O cruelest thief! That steal’st with silent tread
The gold from youth’s bright hair, the fire from maid,
And leaves but ashes where the flame was laid,
And echoes where the music once was spread.

Yet lo! The earth, though stript of summer’s pride,
Still wears a robe of green in winter’s reign,
And though the rose must wither where it grew,
The root lives on, and spring shall yet abide.
So too doth beauty, though it seem but vain,
In nature’s book immortal lines renew.
