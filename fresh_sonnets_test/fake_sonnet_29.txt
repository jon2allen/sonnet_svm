**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the mead with gold ere noon be done,
Why dost thou mock the bloom that fades too soon,
And leave the earth to mourn the vanished sun?
The lark that sang at dawn now silent lies,
The brook that laughed in silver tones is still,
And time, that carves his name on all we fill,
Doth turn our joy to dust before our eyes.

Yet in thy haste, thou cruel thief of days,
Thou leav’st behind the echo of thy flight—
The violet’s breath, the star’s unchanging gaze,
The whispered vow the wind repeats at night.
So take my youth, but leave this truth to me:
That beauty lives where time cannot decree.
