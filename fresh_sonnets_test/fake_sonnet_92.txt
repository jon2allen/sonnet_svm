Upon the Breath of Fleeting Days

O fleeting time, thou thief of golden hours,
That steal’st the rose from youth’s untainted cheek,
And bid’st the violet fade in shadowed bowers,
Whilst silver threads in sable tresses seek.
The lark, that once did greet the dawn’s first light,
Now droops her wing ‘neath evening’s pallid hue,
And streams that laughed in noon’s unclouded sight
Now murmur low to twilight’s fading view.

Yet mark how beauty, though by time undone,
Doth leave her ghost in nature’s endless frame—
The star that pales before the rising sun,
The leaf that falls to feed the bud’s new claim.
So too my love, though time thy bloom may mar,
Shall live in verse, outshining time’s dark star.
