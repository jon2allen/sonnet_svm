Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours in silent haste do glide,
The rose, once fair, now droops her head to weep,
As time’s unkind hand plucks her pride aside.
The brook that sang so sweet in summer’s prime
Now murmurs low beneath the frost’s cold breath,
And all the woods, that once in verdant rhyme
Did chant of life, now whisper tales of death.

Yet mark how dawn still paints the eastern sky,
And stars, though veiled, still hold their ancient light;
So beauty fades not, though it pass and die,
But sleeps in time’s vast ever-turning night.
For what is lost in mortal sight below,
Lives on in heaven’s unchanging, endless glow.
