Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours in silent haste do glide,
The rose, once flushed with youth’s unblushing pride,
Now droops her head where time hath laid his sweep.
O fleeting grace! O beauty’s borrowed keep,
That like the dew beneath the morning’s tide
Doth melt away, and in the wind doth hide—
A breath, a sigh, a dream the soul doth weep.

Yet mark how nature, in her endless round,
Doth spin new threads from old, and weaves anew
The green of spring where winter’s frost was found.
So too the heart, though time its bloom undo,
May find in memory’s light a sweeter ground,
Where love and loss in one bright star do shine.
