When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn gives way to evening’s dew.

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low of summers long since past,
While winds that kissed the meadow’s melody
Are hushed, and all their sweetest songs outcast.

Yet in the hush, where shadows softly creep,
A ghost of beauty lingers, pale and fair,
As though the earth in slumber held it deep,
And time, that thieves all else, spares beauty there.

So take this hour, ere twilight claims the sky—
For love and light must fade, yet never die.
