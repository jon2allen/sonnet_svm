**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal,
Like whispered verse from time’s unyielding hand,
Thy breath doth kiss the rose with tender zeal,
Yet leaves her cheek to pale in death’s command.

The brook, once bright with laughter’s silver thread,
Now murmurs low of joys it may not keep,
While shadows lengthen where the sun hath fed,
And stars awake to watch the earth in sleep.

What alchemy of light and leaf could stay
The cruel swift feet of hours that flee too soon?
The lark, the lily—all must fade away,
As dusk devours the blush of waking noon.

Yet in this loss, a truth doth softly rise:
That beauty lives where time itself dies.
