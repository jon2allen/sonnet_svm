When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
The brook still sings, though youth’s bright fire is through,
And shadows stretch where once the sun stood tall.

O fleeting bloom! O transient, sweet delight,
That fades ere noon can kiss the morning’s face,
Yet in thy passing dost outshine the night,
And leave thy ghost in memory’s embrace.

The oak, grown old, still whispers to the breeze,
Of storms it weathered and of springs it knew,
While I, who marked thee in thy brief bright ease,
Must watch thee fade as all fair things must do.

So take my verse, though time shall wear it thin,
And let thy echo live when thou art kin.
