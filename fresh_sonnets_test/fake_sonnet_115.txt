**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, where dost thou hide thy face?
When dawn did break in robes of crimson hue,
And time, that thief, did steal with silent pace,
Leaving but shadows where thy brightness grew.

The rose, once proud, now droops her weary head,
Her petals kissed by autumn’s chilling breath,
While summer’s song in hollow echoes spread,
A dirge for beauty lost to time and death.

Yet mark the brook that winds through meadows fair,
Though seasons change, its silver course remains,
As though some hand had fixed its path with care,
And taught it to outlast the fleeting stains

Of mortal things. So too may love endure,
Though all else fades—the truest, most pure.
