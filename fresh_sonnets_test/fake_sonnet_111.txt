**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on silver streams,
Whose laughter wakes the rose from slumber deep,
Yet steal too soon, as fleeting as my dreams,
And leave the earth in sorrow’s wintry keep.
The lily fades, the violet bends its head,
The lark’s sweet song is lost to evening’s sigh,
And time, that cruel and careless thief, hath sped,
To rob the world of all it holds most high.

Yet in thy face, fair spring, I see the light
That mocks the grave and scorns the hand of years,
A beauty born of heaven’s purest sight,
That neither age nor time nor grief nor tears
Can mar or dim, though all the world decay—
For love outlives the sun’s last dying ray.
