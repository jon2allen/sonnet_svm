**Sonnet of Fleeting Light**

O golden hour, too swift upon the wing,
Whose gilded touch doth wake the slumbering rose,
Thou paint’st the meads where zephyrs softly close,
And lend’st the brook a voice to softly sing.
Yet lo! the sun, that monarch of the day,
Doth steal thy hue and leave thee pale, forlorn,
As time, that thief, doth bear thy glories borne
Away to dust where no sweet blossoms stay.

The lily fades, the violet droops her head,
The lark’s clear song is lost to evening’s sigh,
And beauty, like the dew on morning spread,
Doth vanish ere the noon-tide draweth nigh.
Yet in my heart thy fleeting grace is laid,
A ghost of joy no winter can invade.
