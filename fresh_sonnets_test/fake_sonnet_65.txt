Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
A fleeting bloom doth kiss the morning’s breath,
Yet lo, the wind, that through the branches spread,
Doth steal the rose ere it can cheat of death.
O cruel hour! That with ungentle hand
Plucks from the stem the jewel of the year,
And leaves but dust where once did brightly stand
The gold that gilded summer’s hemisphere.
Yet mark the brook, that murmurs as it flows,
How it doth bear the sky’s reflected face,
Though ripples twist the light to transient glows,
And time dissolves each borrowed hue and grace.
So too my love, though beauty fades from sight,
Lives in the verse that mocks the thief of night.
