**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on silver streams,
Whose laughter wakes the rose from slumber deep,
Yet steal the bloom ere noon hath crowned thy beams,
And leave the thorn where once the petals sleep.
The lark that soars at dawn’s first trembling light
Now folds her wing where twilight’s shadows creep,
And time, that thief of all we hold most bright,
Doth turn to dust the joys we long to keep.

The river hums a dirge for summer’s reign,
The oak, once clad in emerald’s proud array,
Now bows his head to winter’s cold disdain,
And all fair things must fade and pass away.
Yet in thy face, though time my heart doth mar,
I see the stars that never leave their star.
