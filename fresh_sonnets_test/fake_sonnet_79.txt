**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the mead with gold of transient light,
Then leav’st the bough to shiver, stark and white,
As winter’s breath doth bid the green adieu.
What hand unseen doth paint the dawn anew,
Yet blots the bloom ere noon hath crowned the height?
What thief is time, that robs both day and night,
And leaves but dust where once the lily grew?

Yet in thy theft, thou dost not steal in vain,
For beauty wakes where thy swift foot hath trod—
The frost’s bright lace, the storm’s tempestuous reign,
The hush where once the linnet sang to God.
So take, O Time, what thou must needs possess,
But leave the echo of thy loveliness.
