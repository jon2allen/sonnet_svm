**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour! that dost in silence steal
The bloom from roses ere the dew be dry,
And paint’st the meadows with thy fading wheel,
Where once the lark did mount the vaulted sky.
What hand may stay thee, Time, so swift, so sly?
Thou carv’st thy name in bark of ancient tree,
Yet sparest not the cheek where roses lie,
Nor stay’st the stream that hurries to the sea.

The violet fades, the lily droops her head,
The swan’s white breast grows dim with winter’s breath,
And love, that once did in mine eyes abide,
Now pales before the spectre grim of death.
Yet in thy flight, though all things thou dost mar,
Thou leav’st the stars—eternal, fixed, afar.
