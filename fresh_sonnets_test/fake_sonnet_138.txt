Upon the brow of time where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose whose crimson lips do fade,
As fleeting as the dew on morning’s bed,
Or stars that in the twilight’s grasp are laid.
O cruel swift hours! That steal with silent tread
The gold from dawn, the violet’s tender shade,
And leave but dust where youth and beauty played,
As winds do strip the oak of summer’s spread.

Yet in thy flight, thou dost not all destroy,
For though the rose must wither, seed remains,
And from the earth new blossoms shall deploy,
As phoenix-like, from ashes spring the gains.
So too shall love, though time its form restrains,
In memory’s garden bloom without alloy.
