When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting spring must yield to winter’s dew.
The brook that laughed in silver, light, and free,
Now murmurs low beneath the weight of years,
And trees that stood in youthful majesty
Bow down their heads with sorrow, drowned in tears.

Yet beauty lingers, though its form may fade,
A ghost of grace in memory’s tender keep,
As stars still shine though daylight’s light hath strayed,
And echoes haunt the vales where lovers weep.
So take this truth, though time may mar thy face—
Love’s fire outlasts the ashes of its place.
