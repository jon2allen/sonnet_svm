Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
The golden hours do steal with silent tread,
And beauty fades as mortal flesh doth shed
Its fleeting bloom, like flowers in sleep’s keep.
The rose, once proud, now droops her crimson head,
The lark’s sweet song is lost to evening’s sweep,
Yet in the hush where twilight’s fingers weep,
A ghost of light doth o’er the meadow spread.

O cruel Time! That with thy scythe dost mow
The fields of youth, and leave but stubble bare,
Yet in thy wake the stars their vigils show,
And nature’s book doth still her secrets share.
For though the rose must wither, and the air
Grow cold, the earth remembers what was fair.
