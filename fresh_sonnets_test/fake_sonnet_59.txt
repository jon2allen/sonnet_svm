Upon the breath of fleeting hours we tread,
Where golden dawns in crimson robes do fade,
And beauty, like the rose in summer’s bed,
Must yield unto the scythe that time hath made.
The brook that laughs in silver, clear, and bright,
Now murmurs low beneath the withered bough,
While shadows stretch their fingers, long and slow,
To claim the fields where once was morning’s light.

O cruelest thief! That steal’st the bloom of years,
And leaves but dust where once did roses stand,
Yet in thy wake, the earth still sings and cheers,
For spring returns at thy unyielding hand.
So take my youth, but leave me this alone—
The fleeting grace that in thy passing shone.
