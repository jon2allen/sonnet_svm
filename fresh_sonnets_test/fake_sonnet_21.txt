**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
The bloom from roses ere their prime be past,
And gild’st the meads with gold too soon outcast,
As though the sun would scorn the earth it fed.
What hand hath wrought this swift and cruel thread,
That binds the spring in winter’s icy chain?
The lark, once loud, now droops with weary vein,
And time, that gave, doth take with ruthless dread.

Yet mark how beauty lingers in the air,
A ghost of summer’s kiss on autumn’s brow,
As though the world would keep what it must spare.
But lo! the frost descends—no plea avow—
And all that was shall fade, as shadows do,
Save love, which time nor tide can e’er undo.
