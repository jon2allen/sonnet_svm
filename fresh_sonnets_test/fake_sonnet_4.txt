**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on time’s swift stream,
Like petals borne upon a wanton breeze,
Ye fade too soon, as though a fleeting dream
That melts beneath the sun’s unyielding ease.

The rose, once fair, now droops her weary head,
Her crimson hue to pale decay consigned,
While summer’s song in hollow echoes fled,
And winter’s breath hath left the world behind.

Yet mark the brook that winds through yonder glade,
Its silver voice still murmurs soft and low,
As though the earth, in slumber deep arrayed,
Doth weave her dreams where time doth come and go.

So too shall beauty, though it fade from sight,
Live on in verse, eternal as the light.
