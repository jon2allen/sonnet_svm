When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn must yield to evening’s dew.

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low of summers long since past,
While winds that kissed the blossoms’ revelry
Are hushed, and all their wanton joys are cast.

Yet in the hush, a voice doth softly plead—
“O linger not, nor mourn the lost delight,
For beauty sleeps in winter’s frozen seed,
And wakes anew in spring’s returning light.”

So take this hour, though time may bid it flee,
For love and bloom are born of transience.
