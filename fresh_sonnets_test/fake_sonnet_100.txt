**Sonnet of the Fleeting Rose**

O golden hour, when dawn doth kiss the dew,
And time, that thief, doth steal with silent tread,
The rose, once fair, now bows her weary head,
Her crimson cheek to pale and ashen hue.
Yet in her fall, she scorns the fleeting view,
For though her bloom must fade, her scent is spread—
A ghost of joy that lingers, softly fed
By zephyrs whispering what the earth once knew.

So too the maid, whose eyes outshone the sun,
Whose laughter rang like silver-throated bells,
Now sees her grace by years in shadow spun.
Yet mark! The stars, though distant, never fell,
And beauty, though it fades, is not undone—
For time devours all, save what love foretells.
