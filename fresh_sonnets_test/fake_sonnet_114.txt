Upon the brow of time, where seasons tread,
The rose, once flush with youth’s unblemished hue,
Now bows her head, by autumn’s breath pursued,
And scatters petals where the cold winds spread.
O fleeting grace! O beauty’s borrowed thread,
That weaves the morn in gold, the noon in blue,
Yet fades ere eve, as stars their light undo,
And leaves the world in twilight’s ashen bed.

The brook that laughed in silver, swift, and clear,
Now murmurs low beneath the weight of years,
Its song a dirge for joys it held so dear.
The oak, once proud, in gnarled repose appears,
Its roots entwined with dust, its branches sere—
Yet time, though stern, still whispers what endears.
