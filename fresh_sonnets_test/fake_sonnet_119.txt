When golden hours like petals drift away,
And time’s relentless hand doth wear the rose,
What once was fair must fade in slow decay,
As summer’s blush to winter’s frost bestows.

The brook that sang in silver-throated glee,
Now whispers low beneath the withered bough,
And stars that burned with steadfast constancy
Grow dim, as though their light must needs bow.

Yet in this fall, a grace doth still abide—
The leaf that flames in crimson ere it dies,
The dawn that paints the sky in hues untried,
The song the thrush sings ‘gainst the closing skies.

So too shall beauty, though it fade from sight,
Live on in memory’s everlasting light.
