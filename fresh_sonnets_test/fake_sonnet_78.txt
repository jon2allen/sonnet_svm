When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
The brook still sings, though youth’s bright fire is through,
And summer’s laugh fades into autumn’s pall.

O fleeting bloom! O transient, fair delight!
The lark ascends, yet soon must yield the sky,
As stars must fade before the morning’s eye,
And all sweet things dissolve in time’s cold sight.

Yet in the hush where twilight weaves her spell,
The oak stands firm, though storms may shake his bough,
And love, once fled, in memory doth allow
A ghost of joy no winter’s breath may quell.

So take this hour, ere time its debt doth claim—
For beauty lives, though all else bow to flame.
