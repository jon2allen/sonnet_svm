When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn gives way to evening’s dew.

The brook that sang through meadows green and wide
Now murmurs low where shadows stretch and creep,
While summer’s laugh, once bold and unconfined,
Is hushed beneath the frost’s unyielding sleep.

Yet in the hush, a voice still lingers clear—
The echo of the lark’s first morning cry,
The scent of thyme the wandering winds bear,
The stars that watch where earth and heaven lie.

So take this hour, though brief its fleeting grace,
For time will turn all beauty to embrace.
