**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hours, that steal with silent tread,
And bear the bloom of youth to withered age,
While golden locks to silver threads are led,
And beauty fades like lines on time’s dark page.

The rose, once proud in crimson’s sweet array,
Now droops her head beneath the autumn’s breath,
And summer’s song gives way to winter’s sway,
As life yields up its triumph unto death.

Yet in the hush where twilight shadows play,
A whispered vow the steadfast stars impart—
That though the rose must fade, the thorn stay,
Love’s fire burns deep within the frozen heart.

So take this truth, though time may mar thy grace:
Thou art but dust—yet heaven holds thy place.
