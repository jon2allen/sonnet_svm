**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting shade of gold that gilds the bough,
Where once the rose did blush in crimson pride,
Now frost doth kiss the leaf that trembling bows,
And time, that thief, hath stolen summer’s tide.
The brook that sang in silver, clear and bright,
Now whispers low beneath the ice’s chain,
While winds that once did dance in warm delight
Now howl in sorrow through the barren plain.

Yet mark thee well, though beauty fades too soon,
And youth’s sweet bloom must yield to winter’s hand,
The earth, though wrapped in death’s unyielding croon,
Shall wake again at spring’s first soft command.
So too the soul, though time may dim its flame,
Shall rise renewed in love’s eternal name.
