Upon the Fleeting Breath of Mortal Hours

O fleeting breath of time, that steals so sly,
The rose’s blush, the lark’s melodious tune,
Thou dost unweave the gold from heaven’s high noon,
And leave the earth in ashen shadows’ sigh.
The brook that laughed in silver, clear and bright,
Now murmurs low beneath the weight of years,
While beauty fades like dew ‘neath morning’s tears,
And youth’s fair fire yields to winter’s night.

Yet mark the oak that stands though storms assail,
Its roots entwined with time’s unyielding hand,
Still clad in green when lesser things decay.
So too the soul, though flesh may turn to sand,
Shall rise where love and memory command,
And bloom eternal in some far-off day.
