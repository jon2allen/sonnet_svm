Upon the Breath of Fleeting Days

O fleeting breath of golden afternoon,
Where sunbeams dance on silver-threaded streams,
And time, that thief, with silent, slyest boon,
Steals youth’s bright hue as though it were but dreams.

The rose, once proud in crimson’s bold array,
Now bows her head beneath the autumn’s sigh,
Her petals fall like tears to mark the day
When beauty fades and all fair things must die.

Yet in the hush of twilight’s tender grace,
When stars awake to kiss the fading light,
I see thee still—thy form, thy time-worn face—
A ghost of spring in winter’s hueless night.

So take this verse, though frail as withered leaf,
To mock the grave and cheat time’s cruel thief.
