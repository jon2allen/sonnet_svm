Upon the brow of time where seasons tread,
The rose, once flushed with youth’s unblemished hue,
Now bows her head, by autumn’s breath bested,
And yields to winter’s touch her faded dew.
O fleeting grace! O nature’s borrowed gold,
That gilds the bough ere frost shall strip it bare,
How swift thy bloom, how sure thy story told—
A sigh, a spark, then lost to empty air.

Yet mark the brook that winds through mossed stone,
Still carving deep though centuries may pass,
Or stars that burn when mortal flesh is gone,
And dawns that rise though empires turn to grass.
So beauty fades, but time, that thief, bestows
A light no night can steal, no winter knows.
