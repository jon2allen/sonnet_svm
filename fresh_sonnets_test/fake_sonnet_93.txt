**Sonnet of Fleeting Hours**

O golden morn, where art thy blushes fled?
The rose, once bright, now withers on the vine,
And time, that thief, hath stole what love hath said,
Leaving but dust where once did beauty shine.

The brook that sang in silver-throated glee
Now murmurs low beneath the willow’s shade,
And youth, that laughed in wanton revelry,
Finds age hath marked the path it once did tread.

Yet still the lark ascends the vaulted sky,
And dawn still paints the east in crimson hue,
Though all we cherish fades and must die,
As seasons turn and years their course pursue.

So take this hour, ere time shall make it naught—
Love’s fleeting bloom, by fleeting time is caught.
