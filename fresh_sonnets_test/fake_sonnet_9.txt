Upon the brow of time where shadows play,
The rose, once flushed with dawn’s unspoiled hue,
Now bends her head where twilight’s fingers strew
The gold that gilds her fall in slow decay.
O fleeting grace! O beauty’s borrowed stay!
The brook that laughed in silver, swift, and true,
Now murmurs low of all it once knew—
How youth, like spring, must yield to winter’s sway.

Yet in the hush where fading petals lie,
A seed takes root where none dare look to see,
And time, that thief who steals the brightest dye,
Sows what shall rise in some yet-distant lea.
So turns the wheel, and so the earth is wise—
To lose is but to lend to newer skies.
