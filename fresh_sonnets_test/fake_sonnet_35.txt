Upon the fleeting breath of golden hours,
When dawn doth kiss the dew with lips so bright,
And time, that thief, in silent, stealing powers,
Doth pluck the rose ere noon shall turn to night.
O fleeting bloom! O transience of grace!
The lily fades, the violet droops its head,
And summer’s song, in melancholy space,
Is but a sigh the autumn winds have fed.

Yet in this fall, this withering of green,
There lies a truth no winter can erase—
The soul of beauty, though the flesh be lean,
Doth linger still in memory’s embrace.
For time may mar the form, yet not the light,
That once did shine so fair in nature’s sight.
