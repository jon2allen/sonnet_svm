Upon the Breath of Fleeting Days

O fleeting hours, that dance on silver streams,
And steal the rose from youth’s unguarded cheek,
Why mock the heart with transitory dreams,
Then leave the soul in time’s relentless shriek?

The lily fades where once it proudly stood,
Its petals kissed by dawn’s first golden light,
Yet ere the sun hath climbed the vaulted wood,
It bows its head in sorrow to the night.

So too doth beauty fade, a borrowed grace,
A fleeting guest that tarries but a while,
Till time, that thief, doth steal each fair embrace,
And leaves the world to mourn in silent style.

Yet in the hush where nature’s tears are shed,
The stars still sing of joys that are not dead.
