**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal
The bloom from cheeks where once the roses lay,
And steal’st the song from lips that wont to kneel
To summer’s breath, now fled with yesterday—

See how the brook, once bright with silver gleam,
Now wears the frost’s pale lace in mocking guise,
While time, that thief who feeds on youth’s sweet dream,
Leaves but the husk where once the spirit flies.

Yet lo! The oak, though stripped of verdant pride,
Stands firm against the gale’s ungentle hand,
And in its roots the seasons’ truth doth hide:
That beauty fades, but strength doth ever stand.

So take this truth, though time may mar thy face—
The earth endures, and so shall love’s embrace.
