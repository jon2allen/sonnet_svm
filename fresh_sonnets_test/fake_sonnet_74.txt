**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on time’s swift stream,
Like petals borne upon the breath of May,
How bright ye gleam, yet fade as in a dream,
Lost to the shade where all fair things decay.
The rose, once proud, now droops her crimson head,
The lark’s sweet song is drowned in evening’s sigh,
And beauty, like the dew at dawn outspread,
Melts where the sun doth climb the vaulted sky.

Yet mark the brook that winds through mossed stone,
Still murmuring of joys it knew of old—
So in my heart thy fleeting grace is shown,
A light that time nor storm may quite enfold.
Though years may steal what love and spring impart,
Thy ghost yet lingers, graven in my heart.
