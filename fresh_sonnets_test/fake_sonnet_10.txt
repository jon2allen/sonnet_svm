Upon the brow of Time, where seasons play,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson hue doth fade,
As whispered winds through silvered leaves persuade
The fleeting hours to steal its youth away.
O cruelest thief! That in thy glassy sway,
Dost turn to dust what once in light was made,
And bid the stars their golden paths invade,
To mark the grave where mortal joys decay.

Yet lo! The brook that laughs in sunlit gleam,
Still bears the sky’s bright face upon its breast,
And though the dawn must yield unto the dream
Of dusk’s embrace, and all things sink to rest,
The earth, that drinks the rain and winter’s frost,
Shall wake again—though beauty may be lost.
