Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours in silent haste do glide,
The rose, once fair, must fade in time’s cold tide,
While zephyrs sigh o’er petals lost in sleep.
O fleeting bloom! O beauty’s fragile keep,
That mocks the hand which seeks thee to abide—
The lark still soars, though dawn’s first blush hath died,
And streams still murmur what the heart doth weep.

Yet mark how nature, in her endless art,
Doth weave anew what time hath torn apart:
The bud that withers shall in spring re-start,
As love, though scorned, still lingers in the heart.
So take this truth, though writ in lines so brief—
All passes, save the soul’s unchanging grief.
