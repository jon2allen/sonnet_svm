**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the mead with gold ere noon be done,
Then leav’st the world to pale and ashen hue,
As though the sun had never kissed the sun.

The lark, that sang at dawn in skies so bright,
Now droops his wing where twilight’s shadows creep;
The brook, that laughed in silver, veils its light,
And murmurs low what waking mortals weep.

Yet mark thee well, though beauty fades like snow,
And time’s harsh hand doth wither all it graces,
The earth, that drinks the rain of heaven’s woe,
Shall wake anew in ever-returning places.

So take this truth, though youth and joy depart—
Love’s fire outlives the embers of the heart.
