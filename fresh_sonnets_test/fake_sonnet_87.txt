When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn must yield to evening’s dew.

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low beneath the willow’s shade,
And youth, that danced with careless revelry,
Finds age hath marked the path it once had made.

Yet in the hush where twilight weaves her spell,
A whispered grace doth linger, soft, untold—
The violet’s breath, the nightingale’s farewell,
The stars that gleam like memories of old.

So take this hour, though time may bid thee part,
For beauty lives where nature lends her art.
