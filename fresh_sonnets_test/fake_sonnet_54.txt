**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, why dost thou steal so fast,
And leave behind thy traces faint and few?
The rose that blooms today in crimson cast
Tomorrow fades to pale and ashen hue.
The brook that laughs in silver, clear, and bright,
Shall murmur low when winter’s breath is near,
And all the woods that now in verdure light
Shall stand in barren grief, their glory sere.

Yet time, that reaves the blossom from the bough,
And dims the sun’s most radiant, loving gaze,
Cannot undo the love that lingers now,
Nor quench the fire that in my heart doth blaze.
For though thy face, like spring, must fade from sight,
Its beauty lives in memory’s endless light.
