**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that dost in silence glide,
And steal the rose from beauty’s blushing cheek,
Whilst golden locks to silver threads do seek,
And time’s harsh hand doth youth’s fair bloom deride.

The lark, that erst did greet the dawn so wide,
Now droops her wing where autumn’s winds do shriek,
And streams that laughed in summer’s sunlit meek
Now whisper low ‘neath winter’s frozen tide.

Yet mark thee well, though years may fade the hue
Of petal soft or love’s once-brightest flame,
The earth still turns, and spring shall rise anew.

So take this truth, though time may mar thy frame:
What beauty fades, the soul shall still pursue,
And nature’s song outlasts the fleeting name.
