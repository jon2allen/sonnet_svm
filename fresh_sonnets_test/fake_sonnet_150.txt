**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the mead with gold of transient light,
Why dost thou mock the bloom that fades from view,
And leave the earth to wither in thy flight?

The lark that soars at dawn in skies so clear,
Now droops his wing when twilight dims the air,
And streams that laughed in silver, bright and near,
Now creep in shadows, slow with silent care.

Yet in thy haste, thou leav’st a trace so sweet—
The violet’s breath, the amber of the bee,
The whispered vow the zephyrs softly meet,
And stars that watch where mortal eyes may see.

So take my days, but leave this truth behind:
That beauty lives where love and time entwine.
