**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on silver streams,
Whose laughter wakes the rose from slumber deep,
Why steal ye thus, as though in mocking dreams,
And leave the earth to weep, the woods to weep?

The lily fades, though once it wore the sun,
And time’s harsh hand doth pluck the violet’s hue;
Yet in the shade where twilight’s threads are spun,
Some ghost of beauty lingers, faint and true.

O cruelest thief! Why dost thou spare the stone,
Yet rob the bloom of all its fragrant grace?
The rock stands firm, while youth and joy are flown,
And time’s own face is but a withered place.

Yet mark thee well—though all must fade and fall,
Love’s echo lives where nature gives her all.
