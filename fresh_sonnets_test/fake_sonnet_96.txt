Upon the Fleeting Breath of Golden Hours

When dawn doth kiss the dew with fingers bright,
And silver streams in whispered hymns do flow,
The rose, once proud in crimson’s bold delight,
Now bows her head to time’s unyielding blow.
O fleeting grace! O beauty’s borrowed hue,
That paints the earth in colors rich and rare,
Yet fades as swiftly as the morning’s dew,
Or stars that pale beneath the sun’s bright glare.

The lark, that soars where heaven’s vaults unfold,
Doth sing of joys that time cannot reclaim,
While autumn’s hand, with fingers cold and old,
Strews gold and scarlet on the forest’s frame.
Yet in this change, one truth doth still remain—
Love’s fire outlasts the ashes of the plain.
