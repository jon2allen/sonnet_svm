Upon the brow of time where roses fade,
And golden hours in silent slumber creep,
The fleeting breath of dawn doth softly shade
The hills where youth and summer’s blush do weep.
O cruelest thief! That steal’st the rose’s hue,
And bid’st the violet in thy shadows pine,
While silver threads through sable locks pursue,
And beauty’s lamp doth flicker, faint, divine.

Yet lo! The brook that winds through meadows green,
Still sings the song that time cannot erase,
Nor winter’s frost the ancient oak hath seen
To bow its head beneath the storm’s embrace.
For though thy hand doth steal the bloom of years,
The soul of nature laughs, and time outwears.
