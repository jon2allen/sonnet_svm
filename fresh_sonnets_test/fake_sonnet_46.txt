**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on time’s swift stream,
Like petals borne upon a wanton breeze,
Ye fade ere noon, a half-remembered dream,
And leave but shadows where the sunlight flees.

The rose, once proud in crimson robes arrayed,
Now droops her head beneath the autumn’s breath,
Her beauty lost to winter’s barren shade,
A fleeting ghost of summer’s fragrant death.

Yet in the hush where silver rivers glide,
And twilight weaves her tapestry of gray,
The earth still sings of joys that will abide—
The frost’s bright kiss, the dawn’s unyielding ray.

So take, fair time, what thou must steal away,
But leave thy echoes in the light of day.
