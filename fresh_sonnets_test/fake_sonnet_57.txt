Upon the Breath of Fleeting Moments’ Flight

O golden hour, too swift in passing by,
Thy tender light doth kiss the blooming rose,
Yet lo! ere noon, thy radiance softly goes,
And leaves but shadows where thy brightness lie.
The lark, that sang at dawn so bold and high,
Now droops his wing where evening’s soft wind blows,
And time, that cruel and careless reaper, mows
The fairest flowers that in his path do lie.

What hand may stay the tide of fading grace,
Or bid the stream of years in silence stand?
The silver brook, once bright with summer’s face,
Now murmurs low through autumn’s withered land.
Yet in the heart, where love and memory trace,
The rose still blooms, though lost to mortal hand.
