**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal
The bloom from cheeks where roses once did dwell,
And steal’st the light from eyes that time would quell,
Yet leaves behind a tale none may conceal.

The brook, once bright with laughter’s silver chime,
Now murmurs low of days too swiftly flown,
While autumn’s hand, with touch both soft and sly,
Doth paint the woods in hues of crimson grown.

What art thou, time, that dost so swiftly glide,
And bear away the joys we hold most dear?
The lily fades, the violet turns to tide,
And beauty’s reign but yields to winter’s spear.

Yet in thy theft, some grace thou dost impart—
The fleeting flame that lights the poet’s heart.
