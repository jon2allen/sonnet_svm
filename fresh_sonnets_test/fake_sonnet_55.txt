When golden hours like fleeting shadows glide,
And time’s relentless hand doth wear the rose,
What once was fair must fade, and beauty close
Her eyes to dawn, where twilight’s hues abide.
The lark that sang at morn in meadows wide,
Now silent lies where autumn’s chill wind blows;
The brook that laughed o’er pebbles smooth and slow,
Now hushed, its silver voice in earth denied.

Yet in the hush, a whisper stirs the air—
The oak that stands though storms may rend the sky,
Still holds the stars within its branches high.
So too doth love, though time may steal the fair,
In memory’s light, where shadows dare not fly,
And beauty lives beyond the fleeting eye.
