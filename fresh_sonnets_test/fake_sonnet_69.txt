**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, where dost thou hide thy face?
When dawn did break in robes of crimson hue,
And time, that thief, did steal with silent pace,
Leaving but shadows where thy brightness grew.

The rose, once fair, now bows her weary head,
Her petals strewn upon the earth below,
As though the wind, in haste to wed the dead,
Had kissed her cheeks and bade her softly go.

Yet still the brook doth murmur as it flows,
And still the lark doth mount the morning sky,
Though all we love the cruel season knows,
And beauty fades where time doth pass her by.

So mark thee well, ere youth to age depart—
Love’s fleeting bloom, and hold it to thy heart.
