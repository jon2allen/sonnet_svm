Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours dissolve in fleeting light,
The rose, once bold, doth bow its head to sleep,
While time, that thief, doth steal its crimson bright.
O cruelest hand that plucks the bloom so sweet,
And leaves the thorn to mock the withered stem!
Yet in the hush where twilight’s fingers meet,
The nightingale still sings her requiem.

The brook, once loud with laughter, murmurs low,
Its silver tongue now hushed by winter’s breath,
And where the lark did carol, high and clear,
The wind alone doth chant a dirge of woe.
Yet mark! The dawn shall break, the earth revive,
And beauty wake, though time would bid it thrive.
