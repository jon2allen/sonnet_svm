Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose whose crimson lips do fade,
As morning’s gold gives way to evening’s shade,
And youth, once bright, must bow to age’s thread.

O fleeting hour! Why dost thou steal so swift
The dew from off the meadow’s emerald face,
Or dim the star that in the twilight’s grace
Once burned so bold, now lost to time’s cruel gift?

Yet in thy haste, thou leav’st a trace behind—
The whispered sigh of winds through autumn’s bough,
The silver thread where moonlight weaves its vow,
And beauty’s ghost that lingers in the mind.

So take thy course, relentless, swift, and blind,
For nature’s hand shall keep what time devours.
