**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal,
Like whispered breath upon the morning’s cheek,
Thy touch doth wake the rose from slumber meek,
Yet ere the noon, thy tender grace doth feel
The weight of time, that none may long conceal.
So fades the bloom where once the lark would speak,
And streams that laughed in silver, darkly leak,
While autumn’s hand doth all thy hues reveal.

Lo, where the oak, in summer’s pride arrayed,
Now bows his head to winter’s icy breath,
And beauty, like the dawn, must yield to shade.
Yet in thy fall, there lingers not a death,
But seed of spring, where time’s own art is laid—
A fleeting ghost, a dream that cheats the grave.
