Upon the breath of fleeting hours we tread,
Where golden dawns in crimson twilight fade,
And beauty, like the rose by time betrayed,
Doth bow her head where once her pride was spread.
The brook that sang in silver-threaded tune
Now murmurs low ‘neath autumn’s withered hand,
The lark that scaled the heavens at noon’s command
Falls mute where winter’s icy fingers croon.

O cruel swift feet that dance on time’s own stage,
Ye steal the bloom from youth’s unguarded cheek,
And turn the green to gray on nature’s page.
Yet in this theft, some grace doth softly speak—
For though the rose must wither, still the thorn
Reminds us that all things to dust are born.
