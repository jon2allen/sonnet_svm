Upon the fleeting breath of golden hours,
Where time doth weave his web with silver thread,
The rose, once proud in summer’s sweetest bowers,
Now bows her head, by autumn’s hand bestead.
O fleeting grace! O beauty’s transient flame,
That lights the world but to consume it so,
As morning’s blush must yield unto the same
Dusk’s veil, where all bright hues forgotten go.

Yet in this fall, this withering of might,
There lies a seed of what hath been before—
The earth, though stripped of verdant green delight,
Shall wake anew when winter’s reign is o’er.
So too the soul, though time its bloom may mar,
Finds life in death, as stars in night’s deep jar.
