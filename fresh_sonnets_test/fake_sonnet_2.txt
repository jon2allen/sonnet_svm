Upon the Breath of Fleeting Hours We Tread

O fleeting breath of golden afternoon,
Where sunbeams dance on silver-threaded streams,
And time, that thief, with silent, slyest schemes,
Doth steal the rose ere it hath fully blown.
The lark, that soars where heaven’s azure gleams,
Now folds her wing ‘gainst twilight’s fading tune,
While shadows stretch like memories of June,
And beauty fades as dusk the world entombs.

Yet mark the oak that stands in stubborn grace,
Whose roots entwine the bones of buried years,
Still clad in green though winter’s touch it fears.
So too doth love outlast the swift embrace
Of time’s cold hand, though youth and joy depart—
For nature’s song lives on within the heart.
