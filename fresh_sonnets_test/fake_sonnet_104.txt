Upon the brow of Time, where seasons play,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson hue doth fade,
As whispered winds through silvered leaves persuade
The fleeting hours to steal its grace away.
O cruelest thief! That in thy haste doth lay
Low beauty’s reign, ere youth hath fully strayed,
And turns to dust what once in light arrayed
Did mock the stars with bright and bold display.

Yet lo! The brook, that laughs in endless flight,
Still sings the song the ancient hills have known,
Though mortal hands may never stay its might.
So too the soul, though bound to flesh and bone,
Shall drink the dawn’s eternal, pure delight—
When Time itself is but a shadow thrown.
