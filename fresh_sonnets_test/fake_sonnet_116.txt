When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn gives way to evening’s dew.
The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low where shadows stretch and creep,
And summer’s song, once sung so blithely,
Is but a sigh the hollow winds repeat.
Yet in this hush where beauty yields to night,
A seed takes root where none dare look to see—
The lark still climbs though day be drowned in light,
And love, like stars, burns steadfast though we flee.
So take this hour, though brief its borrowed grace,
For time may steal the bloom, but not the place.
