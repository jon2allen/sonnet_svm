Upon the cheek of Time, where roses fade,
And golden locks to silver threads do turn,
There Nature weeps in hues of green and shade,
As fleeting hours her tender works adjourn.
The lark that once did greet the dawn’s first light
Now sings a dirge for summers long since past,
While autumn’s breath, both keen and cold in might,
Doth strip the boughs where blossoms clung steadfast.

O cruelest thief! That steal’st the bloom of May,
And leaves in wake the barren, frost-kissed bough,
Yet in thy theft, some grace thou canst not slay—
The echo of the rose that lingers now.
For though the petal falls, the root remains,
And love, like spring, shall rise from winter’s chains.
