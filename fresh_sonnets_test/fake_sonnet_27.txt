Upon the Breath of Fleeting Hours We Tread

When golden dawn doth kiss the waking earth,
And dew like scattered pearls adorns the green,
A fleeting glance of beauty’s tender birth,
That time, unkind, shall steal ere day be seen.
The rose, once proud in crimson robe arrayed,
Doth bow her head beneath the reaper’s hand,
And youth, that laughed in wanton lightness played,
Now fades as leaves before the autumn’s brand.

O cruel swift hours! Why must ye thus devour
The bloom of spring, the summer’s fervent glow?
Why steal the song that charms the halcyon hour,
And leave but echoes where the sweet winds blow?
Yet in thy theft, some grace thou dost impart—
For beauty lives, though time may break the heart.
