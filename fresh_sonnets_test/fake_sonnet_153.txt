**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st with silent tread,
And bear’st the bloom from beauty’s blushing cheek,
Whilst golden locks to silver threads are wed,
And time doth carve his name on brow so meek.

The rose, once proud in summer’s wanton prime,
Now droops her head beneath the autumn’s breath,
And all the joys that dance within her rhyme
Are lost unto the hollow halls of death.

Yet mark thee well, though seasons fade and fall,
And youth’s bright fire to embers turns at last,
The earth, that mourns her children one and all,
Shall wake anew when winter’s spell is past.

So take this truth, though time may steal thy days—
Beauty but sleeps, and wakes in nature’s praise.
