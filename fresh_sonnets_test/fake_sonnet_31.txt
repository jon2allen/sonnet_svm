When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
The brook still sings, though youth’s bright fire is through,
And dawn’s first blush must fade to evening’s pall.

O fleeting bloom! O light too soon undone,
That once did dance upon the meadow’s breast,
Now withered lies beneath the waning sun,
A ghost of joy where weary shadows rest.

Yet in the hush where twilight weaves her spell,
The earth remembers what the heavens know—
That beauty, though it fade, doth not farewell,
But sleeps in roots where spring’s green fingers grow.

So time may turn, and seasons drift away,
But nature’s song outlasts the briefest day.
