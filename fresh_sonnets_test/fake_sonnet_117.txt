Upon the fleeting breath of golden hours,
Where time doth weave his web in silver thread,
The rose, once proud in summer’s courtly bowers,
Now bows her head, by autumn’s hand bested.
O fleeting grace! O beauty’s borrowed hue,
That paints the mead in colors bright and vain,
Yet fades as swift as morning’s pearled dew
When noon’s hot gaze doth scorch the earth again.

The brook that sang in merry, murmuring rhyme
Now whispers low of days forever fled,
While trees, once clad in emerald prime,
Stand bare and bleak where winter’s chill is spread.
Yet in this hush, where time his tale hath told,
Love’s root runs deep, though all above grows old.
