**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal
The bloom from cheeks where roses once did dwell,
And steal’st the breath from lips that time would quell,
Yet leave’st the trace of what we once did feel.

The brook that laughed in silver, bright and clear,
Now murmurs low with age’s whispered sigh,
While autumn’s hand, once warm, now chills the sky,
And strips the bough where spring’s first buds appear.

Yet in this waste, where beauty fades to dust,
A fleeting grace doth linger in the air—
The echo of thy voice, the sun’s last glare,
The ghost of love that time cannot adjust.

So take my hand, ere youth and joy depart,
And kiss the shade that lingers in my heart.
