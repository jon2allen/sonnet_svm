**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours, that dance on time’s swift stream,
Like petals borne upon a wanton breeze,
Ye fade too soon, as though but waking dreams,
Or stars that drown in daylight’s pale decrees.

The rose, once flushed with summer’s bold embrace,
Now droops her head where frost hath kissed the thorn,
And youth, that laughed in beauty’s fleeting grace,
Finds age hath marked his brow with silver scorn.

Yet still the lark at dawn doth mount the sky,
And still the brook doth murmur through the glade,
As though to mock the tears that dim mine eye,
And scorn the sighs that time hath ill repaid.

So take, fair Spring, this heart thou didst beguile—
Thy joys are brief, yet sweeter for their guile.
