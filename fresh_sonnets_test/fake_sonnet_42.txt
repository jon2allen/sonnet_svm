**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on zephyr’s wing,
When dawn unfolds her robe of amber light,
And blossoms wake to hear the lark’s sweet sing,
While earth doth wear her mantle fresh and bright—
Yet lo! how swift the fleeting moments glide,
As shadows stretch where once the sunbeams played.
The rose, once fair, must fade by time’s cold tide,
And beauty’s bloom in silent dust is laid.

The brook that laughed in silver-tongued delight
Now murmurs low of joys it may not keep,
And autumn’s touch, with fingers wan and white,
Steals summer’s crown ere she can wake from sleep.
Yet in this change, a truth doth still abide:
Love’s fire outshines the ashes time hath made.
