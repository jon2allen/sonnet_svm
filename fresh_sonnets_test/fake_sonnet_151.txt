When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting spring must yield to winter’s dew.
The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low beneath the weight of years,
While oaks that stood so proud in majesty
Bow down their heads, and drop their autumn tears.

Yet in this change, a constant truth is spun—
That beauty, though it wither, lives anew,
For every dawn must kiss the dying sun,
And every end gives birth to morning’s dew.
So take this fleeting world with gentle grace,
For time, though stern, still spins the stars in place.
