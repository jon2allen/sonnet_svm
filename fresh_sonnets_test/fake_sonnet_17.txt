**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hours that dance on time’s swift stream,
Like petals borne upon a wanton breeze,
Ye fade too soon, a half-remembered dream,
And leave but shadows where your brightness flees.
The rose, once proud in crimson summer’s reign,
Now bows her head to autumn’s chilling breath,
Her beauty lost to winter’s frost and rain,
A ghost of joy that lingers near in death.

Yet mark how spring, with tender hand, restores
The earth’s fair face, as though no time had passed,
And bids the brook in silver laughter pours,
While larks ascend where twilight’s hues are cast.
So too shall fade the bloom that now we prize,
But time, in turn, shall wake it to the skies.
