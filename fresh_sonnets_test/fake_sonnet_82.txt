Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson lips confess
The fleeting grace of all that must possess
The earth’s embrace, yet soon to dust be sped.
O cruel hourglass! Thy sands do measure
The span of joys that fade like morning’s dew,
While golden suns in vaulted heavens strew
Their light on fields where youth and love do treasure.

Yet mark thee well, though beauty’s lamp may wane,
And silver threads through raven locks be spun,
The soul’s fair spring in memory doth remain,
As stars outlive the day when day is done.
For nature’s hand, though time may steal her art,
Still paints the sky when beauty leaves the heart.
