When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting as the morning’s tender dew.
The brook that sang in silver-throated glee
Now murmurs low where shadows deeper creep,
And summer’s laugh, so wild and bold and free,
Is but a ghost where autumn winds weep.

Yet still the earth, in slumber wrapped so tight,
Doth dream of dawns when all was fresh and fair,
When love and light did dance in pure delight,
And beauty wore no mask of time’s despair.
So take this hour, though brief its fleeting stay—
For time devours, yet art may bid decay.
