**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O golden hour, that dost in silence steal
The bloom from cheeks where roses once did dwell,
And steal’st the song from lips that sweetly fell,
As time’s unkind hand makes the fair to reel.

The brook, that laughed in silver-tressed glee,
Now murmurs low beneath the withered bough,
And stars, that once in heaven’s vault did glow,
Pale in the dawn’s relentless mockery.

Yet mark how yonder oak, though bent with age,
Still lifts its arms to greet the morning’s light,
Whilst youth, like dew upon the meadow’s stage,
Doth vanish ere the noon hath reached its height.

So take this truth, though beauty fades too soon:
The earth endures, but not the fleeting moon.
