Upon the fleeting breath of golden hours,
Where time doth weave his web with silver thread,
The rose, once proud in summer’s blushing bowers,
Now bows her head, by autumn’s chill bested.
O cruel swift feet! That dance on beauty’s grave,
And steal the hue from lips once ruby-bright,
As streams that kiss the shore they cannot save,
Yet leave the bank to wither in their flight.

The lark, that sang of dawn in skies so clear,
Now hushes low where twilight’s shadows creep,
And all the joys that once to me were dear
Lie wrapt in sleep, or lost in time’s vast deep.
Yet in this loss, a truth doth softly rise—
That beauty lives, though mortal form decays,
For nature’s hand, with ever-watchful eyes,
Renews the earth in never-fading ways.
