Upon the dial’s face where shadows creep,
And golden hours in silent haste do glide,
The rose, once flushed with youth’s unblushing pride,
Now bows her head to time’s unyielding sweep.
O fleeting bloom! O beauty’s brief delight!
Like morning’s dew that melts at noon’s first ray,
Or stars that fade before the break of day,
Thou dost but mock the gaze of mortal sight.

Yet in thy fall, a sweeter grace is spun—
The leaf that trembles ere it leaves the bough,
The twilight’s blush when day is nearly done,
The song the thrush sings ere the night doth vow.
For time, though cruel, doth teach the heart to know
That love outlives the rose, and stars the snow.
