When golden hours like petals drift away,
And time’s relentless hand doth mark the air,
What once was bright now fades in pale decay,
As fleeting blooms dissolve in autumn’s snare.

The rose, that kissed the morn with crimson grace,
Now bows her head beneath the evening’s sigh,
While silver streams, once swift in youthful chase,
Now creep with age ‘neath heaven’s watchful eye.

Yet in this hush where beauty yields to rust,
A whispered vow the steadfast stars impart—
That though the flesh must wither into dust,
The soul of spring lives on in nature’s art.

So take this truth, though time may steal the hue:
What dies in form, in essence blooms anew.
