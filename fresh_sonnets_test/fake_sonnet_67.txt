When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time, that thief, doth steal the rose’s hue,
The brook still sings, though youth’s bright fire is through,
And dawn’s first blush must fade to evening’s pall.

O fleeting bloom! O transient, sweet delight,
That once did dance upon the meadow’s breast,
Now withered lies beneath the pale moon’s light,
While stars above in silent splendour rest.

Yet in the hush where twilight’s shadows play,
The earth still breathes with memories of thee—
The violet’s sigh, the lark’s last song at day,
The whispering wind that wanders through the lea.

So take this truth, though beauty fades too soon:
Nature’s own hand doth weave thee into tune.
