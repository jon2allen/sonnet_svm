**Sonnet of the Fleeting Rose**

O golden hour when dawn doth kiss the mead,
And silver brooks in murmuring whispers run,
The rose, once proud, now bows her weary head,
While time, unkind, steals all that she hath won.
Her crimson blush, so fair in morning’s light,
Doth fade as twilight yields to sable night,
And petals fall like dreams forgot ere noon—
So fades the bloom that charmed the sun at noon.

Yet in her fall, a sweeter grace is spun,
For earth reclaims what heaven once did lend,
And from her dust new life shall rise anew,
When spring returns to wake the sleeping end.
Thus beauty dies, yet in her dying breath,
She mocks the grave and scorns the hand of death.
