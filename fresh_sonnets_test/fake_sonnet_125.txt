**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the mead with gold of transient light,
Why dost thou mock the bloom that fades from view,
And leave the earth to shroud in winter’s night?

The lark, that sang at dawn in skies so clear,
Now droops his wing ‘neath evening’s pallid breath;
The brook, that laughed in silver, runs in fear,
And whispers tales of time that conquereth.

Yet mark!—where shadows creep on mossed stone,
A violet lifts its head in trembling grace,
As if to say, though all must fade and groan,
Some spark endures in nature’s fleeting face.

So take, sweet time, the gold from out my hand,
But leave the bloom thou canst not understand.
