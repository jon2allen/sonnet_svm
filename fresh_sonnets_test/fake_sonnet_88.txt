When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn yields to the evening’s dew.

The brook that sang in silver-throated glee
Now murmurs low where shadows stretch and creep,
And summer’s laugh, so wild and bold and free,
Is but a ghost the hollow winds will keep.

Yet in the hush where twilight bends to night,
A single star still trembles, pale and clear,
As though some lost and luminous delight
Might yet return if memory held it near.

So take this hour, this breath, this fleeting light—
For beauty dies, and time devours all here.
