Upon the Breath of Fleeting Days

O fleeting hours, that dance on silvered wing,
Through golden fields where youth and beauty play,
You steal the rose ere yet it learns to cling,
And bid the lark forget the break of day.
The brook that laughed in crystal, clear delight,
Now murmurs low of shadows yet to be,
While time, that thief of all we hold so bright,
Doth carve his name where once was jollity.

Yet mark the oak, whose roots in earth entwine,
Though storms may shake his branches, bold and free,
He stands unbowed, as though in scorn divine,
And scorns the gnawing tooth of destiny.
So too the soul, though beauty fade and fall,
Shall rise anew where time doth fear to crawl.
