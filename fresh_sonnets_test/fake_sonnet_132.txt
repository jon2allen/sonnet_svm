Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson lips do part
With whispers soft as twilight’s fleeting art,
Yet fades ere dawn may kiss the tears it shed.
O fleeting grace! Like streams that downward sped,
Or silver clouds that drift on winds apart,
Thy beauty lingers but to break the heart,
A ghost of joy in memory’s garden spread.

The lark still sings where golden hours have flown,
And violets weep where once thy footsteps played,
While autumn’s hand, with ruthless touch, is laid
On summer’s crown of emerald and throne.
Yet in the hush where time’s cold breath is blown,
Love’s echo stays—though all else fade and fade.
