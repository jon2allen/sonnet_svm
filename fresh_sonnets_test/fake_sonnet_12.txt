When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting dawn must yield to evening’s dew.

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low of summers long outworn,
While winds that kissed the meadow’s tapestry
Now sigh through branches bare and winter-torn.

Yet in this hush where beauty seems to die,
A seed takes root where none had deemed it so—
The same sun that once lit the summer sky
Shall wake the earth when winter’s frost shall go.

So trust the turn, though time may steal the bloom,
For spring is but the phoenix of the tomb.
