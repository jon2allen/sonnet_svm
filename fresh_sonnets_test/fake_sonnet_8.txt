When golden hours, like petals soft and bright,
Do drift upon the breath of fleeting air,
The rose, once proud in summer’s fervent light,
Now bows her head in sorrow, thin and spare.
O cruelest thief! That steals with silent tread,
The bloom of youth, the blush of morning’s face,
And leaves but dust where once the roses fed,
And time’s harsh mark on every tender grace.

Yet in the hush of twilight’s fading glow,
When dusk descends with gentle, whispered sigh,
The stars awake where once the sun did flow,
And paint the heavens with their endless eye.
So too shall beauty, though it fade from sight,
Live on in memory’s everlasting light.
