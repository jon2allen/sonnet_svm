**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the meadow with thy golden tread,
What art thou but a thief in heaven’s bed,
Who robs the bloom ere youth itself is through?
The lark, that mounts at dawn with songs so true,
Now droops his wing where autumn’s shadows spread,
And time, that once did kiss the violet’s head,
Now scorns the leaf that trembles, pale of view.

Yet mark how beauty, though it fade and fall,
Doth leave its ghost upon the waking air—
The echo of a laugh, the rose’s pall,
The sigh that lingers where the sweet was fair.
So in thy flight, O time, thou dost not kill,
But bid the world remember what was still.
