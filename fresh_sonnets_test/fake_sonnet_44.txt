**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And gild’st the mead with gold of transient light,
Then leav’st the world to shadows, cold and bright,
As beauty fades and time doth all subdue.
The lark that sings at dawn in skies so blue,
The brook that laughs in silver, swift in flight,
The bloom that bends to kiss the morning’s sight—
All yield to thee, relentless, stern, and true.

Yet in thy haste, thou dost not spare the heart
That clings to joys as dew clings to the leaf,
Nor stay the hand that plucks love’s tender part.
But lo! Though time may mar both joy and grief,
The earth still turns, the stars their courses keep,
And beauty sleeps in nature’s endless deep.
