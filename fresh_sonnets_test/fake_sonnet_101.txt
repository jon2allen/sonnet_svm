When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting as the morning’s tender dew.
The brook that sang in silver-throated glee,
Now murmurs low where shadows deepen fast,
And summer’s bloom, once bold in revelry,
Must yield to autumn’s melancholy blast.

Yet in this change, a quiet grace remains—
The oak that stands though storms may shake its bough,
The star that gleams through night’s unyielding chains,
The love that time nor tide can overthow.
So take this truth, though beauty fades too soon:
Its ghost still lingers in the pale-faced moon.
