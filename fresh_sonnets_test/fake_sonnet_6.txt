When golden hours like petals softly fall,
And time’s swift hand doth steal the rose’s hue,
What once was bright now fades beyond recall,
As fleeting spring must yield to winter’s dew.

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and free,
Now murmurs low beneath the weight of years,
And youth, that danced on wings of ecstasy,
Finds age has dimmed the light of former cheers.

Yet in the hush where twilight bends to night,
A whispered grace still lingers in the air—
The stars, though old, still kindle heaven’s light,
And beauty sleeps where time cannot repair.

So take this truth, though time may mar the bloom:
Love’s fire outlasts the ashes of the tomb.
