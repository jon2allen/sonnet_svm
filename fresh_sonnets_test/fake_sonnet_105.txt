Upon the Breath of Fleeting Moments’ Flight

O fleeting hour, that steal’st the rose’s hue,
And dims the gold where dawn did first unfold,
Thy silent step doth bid the meadows old
To yield their green to winter’s ashen view.
Yet mark! where petals tremble, kissed by dew,
A fleeting grace doth in their blush behold—
As though the earth, in sorrow half consoled,
Would stay thy hand with beauty ever new.

But lo! the brook that laughed in silver tone
Now murmurs low beneath the withered bough,
And time, that spares nor stone nor star alone,
Doth carve his name where youth and joy did glow.
Yet in thy theft, thou cruel, art not shrewd—
For beauty lives where thou canst not pursue.
