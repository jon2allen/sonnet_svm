Upon the breath of fleeting hours we tread,
Where golden dawns in crimson robes do fade,
And beauty, like the blossom softly spread,
Doth yield unto the scythe of time’s parade.
O fleeting sprite! Why dost thou thus decay,
When summer’s kiss still lingers on thy cheek?
The brook that sang thee lullabies all day
Now murmurs low, its voice grown faint and meek.

Yet mark the oak that stands in silent grace,
Whose roots delve deep where mortal eyes ne’er see,
Though seasons carve their story on his face,
He bends not to the wind’s inconstancy.
So too thy soul, though time may mar thy frame,
Shall in the stars eternal burn the same.
