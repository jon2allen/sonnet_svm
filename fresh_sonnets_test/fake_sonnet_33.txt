Upon the brow of Time, where seasons tread,
There blooms a rose, whose crimson lips do part
To whisper secrets to the listening heart,
Yet ere the morrow’s dawn, its hues are shed.
The silver brook, with murmurs soft and low,
Doth carve its path through meadows green and fair,
But fleeting as the breath of evening air,
Its song is lost where twilight’s shadows grow.

O fleeting grace! O transient, sweet delight!
Like golden leaves that dance upon the breeze,
Or stars that fade before the sun’s first light,
Thou dost elude the grasp of mortal pleas.
Yet in the hush of dusk, when day is done,
Thy ghost remains, a dream half-seen, half-sung.
