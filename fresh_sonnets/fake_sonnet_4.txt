Upon the dial’s face where shadows play,
The fleeting hours do carve their hasty flight,
As golden beams dissolve to ashen gray,
And day yields up her crown to starry night.
O cruelest thief! That steal’st the rose’s hue,
And bid’st the lily wither on the stem,
While time, unkind, doth bid the world adieu,
And leaves no trace where once it smiled on them.

Yet in the grove where ancient oaks do stand,
And whisper secrets to the listening air,
There lingers still a beauty, soft yet grand,
That mocks the scythe which all else doth ensnare.
For though the bloom may fade, the root runs deep,
And time’s own hand cannot the soul’s song keep.
