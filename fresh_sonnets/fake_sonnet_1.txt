Upon the brow of Time, where seasons play,
There blooms a rose whose crimson lips do fade,
As fleeting as the dawn’s first golden shade,
Or silver streams that hasten to the bay.
O cruelest thief! That steal’st the breath away,
And leav’st the hollow shell where once was laid
The fairest form that ever mortal made,
Now dust, where once did dance the light of day.

Yet mark thee well, though beauty’s span be brief,
And all must yield unto the scythe’s cold sway,
The earth, that drinks the tears of heaven’s grief,
Doth weave from death a tapestry of gray.
For though the rose must wither, fall, and die,
Its ghost in petals haunts the April sky.
