**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting breath of time, that steals so sly,
The rose’s blush, the lily’s snowy grace,
And leaves but dust where youth and beauty lie,
A withered wreath in some forgotten place.

The brook that laughed in silver, bright, and clear,
Now murmurs low beneath the willow’s shade,
While golden hours dissolve in yesteryear,
And love, once bold, in silent grief is laid.

Yet mark the dawn, how fresh the light doth break,
And paint the heavens with hues of fire and gold,
As though the earth her ancient joys would wake,
And bid the heart its fleeting joys behold.

So take this hour, ere time its debt exact,
For beauty fades, and love must turn to fact.
