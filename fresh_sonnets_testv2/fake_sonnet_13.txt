**Sonnet of the Alchemist’s Ledger**

The crucible doth hiss where mercury
Dissolves the base—yet no philosopher’s stone
Shall turn this dross to coin, though I decree
The fire’s harsh lease should yield a weight full-grown.

What notary attests the vapor’s flight,
Or seals the retort’s breath in waxed decree?
No writ may bind the quicksilver’s bright spite,
Nor audit fix what flees the alchemist’s fee.

The alembic, once clear as herald’s glass,
Now clouds with fumes that mock the scribe’s neat hand;
The furnace, stoked to judge the ore’s dark mass,
Doth spew forth slag where no true gold doth stand.

Yet still I melt, though transmutation’s vain—
For love’s the debt no crucible can drain.
