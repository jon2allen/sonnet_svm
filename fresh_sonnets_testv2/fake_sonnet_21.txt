**Sonnet of the Shipwright’s Bond**

Shall I thy keel in doubtful pitch embalm,
Or trust the tide that gnaws the oak’s green grain?
The chandler’s wax, though sealed with care, may calm
No worm that bores where hidden currents reign.

The pilot’s log, though writ in ink most true,
Doth falter when the leeward shoals arise—
Yet thou dost steer by reckoning ever new,
Where no man’s chart hath ta’en the sun’s devise.

What crucible could melt such stubborn ore
As patience in thy silence, deep and wide?
The notary’s quill, the herald’s crest no more
Endure, but thou dost stand where all abide.

Then take this spar, though warped by storm and sun—
The sea’s last bond is sworn in thee alone.
