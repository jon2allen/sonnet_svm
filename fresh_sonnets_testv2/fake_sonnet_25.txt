**Sonnet of the Reeve’s Reckoning**

What audit marks the yield when frost hath gnawed
The stubble where the reaper’s sickle passed?
No tally-stick nor steward’s ledger sawed
Can count the loss when winter’s grip is cast.

The plowman’s ox, though yoked with patient might,
Doth falter when the furrow turns to stone—
Yet thou dost break the clod with love’s own light,
And coax the grain where none had thought to sown.

What feoffment grants the right to hold so dear
A heart that time nor blight can rend asunder?
The bailiff’s writ, the herald’s blazon clear,
Fade like the chaff when love’s true measure’s pondered.

Then take this sheaf, though scant the harvest be—
The reeve’s last sum is writ in constancy.
