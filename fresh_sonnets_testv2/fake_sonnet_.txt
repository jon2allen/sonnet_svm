**Sonnet of the Tiller’s Audit**

O let me not thy furrowed lease enfeoff
To th’ wanton winds that mar the drilled row—
Each clod a bond, each seed a writ below,
Where time’s stern audit doth the yield reprove.
Yet lo, the plowshare’s edge, once bright, doth groove
A paler path where rust doth gnaw and grow,
As if the soil, in mutinous overthrow,
Had sworn to keep what labor strove to move.

What alchemist could turn this leaden yield
To grain that swells like pennants in the gale?
The scythe, once keen, now falters, dull with toil,
And harvest’s ledger ne’er shall balance pale.
Yet still I till, though th’ earth withhold its spoil—
For love’s the lease no audit can despoil.
