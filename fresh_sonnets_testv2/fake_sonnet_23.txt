**Sonnet of the Navigator’s Quill**

What star doth guide thee when the compass fails,
And polar light is swallowed by the deep?
No lodestone’s touch nor mariner’s old tales
Can chart the shoals where memory’s currents sweep.

The astrolabe, though wrought with cunning art,
Doth falter when the heavens veil their face—
Yet thou dost steer by some unerring part
Of time’s own tide, though tempests shift the place.

What notary could witness such a bond
As love that holds when anchors drag and break?
The admiral’s seal, the pilot’s log are gone,
But thou dost stand where no man’s course can take.

Then take this log, though blotted, torn, and worn—
The sea’s last reckoning is love’s true morn.
