**Sonnet of the Husbandman’s Ledger**

Dost thou not see how autumn’s scythe doth shear
The stubble left where summer’s tithe was paid?
No bond is writ in th’ earth but time shall tear,
Nor lease of soil but winter’s grip hath swayed.

The reeve’s account, though tallied line by line,
Must reckon with the blight that mars the sheaf—
Yet thou dost stand where harvests still outshine
The rot that claims the barn, the bin, the leaf.

What alembic holds the fire that forges
A love unyielding as the plowman’s call?
The herald’s blazon fades, the law’s writ gorges
Itself on dust, but thou dost outlast all.

Then take this yield, though gleaned in want and dearth—
The ledger’s sum is love’s unmeasured worth.
