**Sonnet of the Shipwright’s Oath**

When first the adze did bite the oak’s green hide,
And ribs arose like writs in some dark court,
I swore the keel should cleave the salted tide—
Yet waves, not law, would prove my true resort.

What alchemist distills the wind’s wild lease,
Or bids the tide obey a carpenter’s hand?
No parchment bond can stay the storm’s increase,
Nor seal the deep where no man’s writ may stand.

The mast, once straight as any herald’s lance,
Now bends beneath the gale’s unyielding claim;
The hull, once sound, doth groan in dire advance,
And rot, not gold, becomes the ship’s true name.

Yet still I build, though timber ne’er be true—
For love’s the craft no tempest can undo.
