**Sonnet of the Shipwright’s Bond**

Shall I compare thee to the keel’s true line,
That parts the brine yet holds the ribs in trust?
No—thou art more, for time’s corrosive rust
Ne’er gnaws the oath where mortise meets the pine.

The caulker’s pitch, though black as usurer’s ink,
Seals not so tight as does thy whispered vow;
Nor does the leaden plumb that sounds the bough
Of sunken reefs so steady make me sink.

What herald’s blazon could thy worth impart,
When even stars, those gilded escutcheons,
Must pale before the compass of thy heart?
The tiller turns, yet thou dost steer my bones.

Then let the waves their ancient writ command—
I’ll drown in thee, not th’ sea’s uncharted sand.
