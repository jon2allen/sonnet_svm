**Sonnet of the Reeve’s Forfeit**

When autumn’s audit strips the tenant’s store,
Thy voice is writ in ledgers of the grain—
No tally-stick nor bailiff’s sharp refrain
Can seize the tithe thou dost withhold before.

The falcon’s hood may blind the hooded eye,
Yet thou dost pierce the murk of reeve’s deceit;
The scythe that parts the stubble at thy feet
Leaves no arrears for serjeant’s writ to try.

What alchemist could cozen time’s harsh toll,
Or turn the dross of toil to herald’s blazon?
The furrow yawns where no man’s plow has gone,
And thou dost walk the ridge with unpaid scroll.

Then let the steward’s quill its ink deny—
I’ll sign my bond in dust thou dost untie.
