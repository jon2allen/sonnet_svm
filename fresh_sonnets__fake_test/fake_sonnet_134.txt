**Sonnet of the Seasons' Dance**

When April's breath doth wake the sleeping rose,
And summer's gold gives place to autumn's hue,
What subtle hand unseen the bloom bestows,
Then steals the light of beauty bright and true?

The lily, once in ivory pride arrayed,
Now bows her head where winter's chill doth creep,
The lark that sang in morning's golden shade
Now silent lies where frost hath sealed her sleep.

Yet mark how silver decks the barren bough,
And paints the mead with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet nature's dance doth bid them rise anew.
