**Sonnet of Time's Gentle Hand**

When dawn's first blush doth kiss the waking earth,
And noon's bright fire yields to twilight's grace,
What subtle thief doth steal each sweet birth,
And leave the world in shadows' cold embrace?

The rose that bloomed in crimson, proud display,
Now droops her head where winter's breath hath blown,
The nightingale that sang the livelong day
Now silent sits on branches bare and lone.

Yet mark how frost doth deck the withered bough,
And paint the mead with silver, pale and bright,
The moon that walks in heaven's starry brow,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet time's own hand doth guard their fading power.
