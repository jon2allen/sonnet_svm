**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

When April's blush hath kissed the waking rose,
And summer's gold doth melt to autumn's gray,
What power unseen doth bid the blossom close,
And steal the light of beauty's bright array?

The lily, once in ivory pride arrayed,
Now droops her head where winter's breath doth creep,
The nightingale, whose song the groves obeyed,
Now silent sits where frost hath sealed her sleep.

Yet mark how silver decks the barren bough,
And paints the mead with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet nature's hand doth bid them rise alway.
