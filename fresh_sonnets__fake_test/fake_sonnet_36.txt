**Sonnet of the Ebbing Tide**

When golden hours like leaves before the blast
Do scatter wide and fade into the air,
What power unseen doth bid the fairest last,
And turn to dust the joys that once were fair?

The rose that bloomed in crimson, proud display,
Now droops her head where winter's breath hath blown,
The nightingale that sang the livelong day
Now silent sits on branches bare and lone.

Yet mark how silver decks the withered bough,
And paints the mead with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that walks in solitary grace,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet time's own hand doth guard their fading power.
