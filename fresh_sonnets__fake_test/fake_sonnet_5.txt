**Sonnet of the Unseen Tide**

O ceaseless tide that through the world doth glide,
Bearing the bloom wherewith the spring was crowned,
What hand unseen doth bid the rose abide,
Then pluck the flower when summer’s joys are drowned?

The lark that soared in morning’s golden light,
Now silent lies where autumn’s breath is cold,
The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and bright,
Now murmurs low beneath the withered wold.

Yet mark the frost that paints the barren bough,
And gilds the mead with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that rules the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet time’s harsh hand doth bear them all away.
