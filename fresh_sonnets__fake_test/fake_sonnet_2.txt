**Sonnet of the Unseen Hand**

When golden noon doth melt to ashen eve,
And time’s swift wing doth brush the trembling rose,
What hand unseen doth bid the blossom leave,
And steal the hue where youth’s bright glory glows?

The river winds, yet never stays its course,
The lark ascends, yet falls to earth again,
And beauty fades, though love should stay its force—
A fleeting guest in sorrow’s endless reign.

Yet mark the oak that stands though storms assail,
The frost that paints the field with silver grace,
The moon that rules the night with ghostly pale,
And stars that watch where mortal shadows pass.

So time shall take what time alone can give,
And teach the heart to mourn, yet bid it live.
