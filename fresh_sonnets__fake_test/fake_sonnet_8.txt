**Sonnet of the Fleeting Span**

When golden hours like leaves before the blast
Do scatter wide and fade into the air,
What power unseen doth bid the fairest last,
And turn to dust the joys that once were fair?

The violet, once in purple pride arrayed,
Now droops her head where chilling dews descend,
The nightingale, whose song the groves obeyed,
Hath hushed her voice where winter's reign doth tend.

Yet lo! the frost that gilds the leafless tree,
And paints the mead with hues of silver bright,
The moon that rules the vaulted canopy,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet nature's hand doth bid them rise alway.
