**Sonnet of the Ebbing Tide**

When summer's flame doth yield to autumn's chill,
And time's relentless hand doth mar the rose,
What sorcery unseen doth bid it still,
And steal the bloom where youth's bright glory grows?

The lark that soared in morning's golden light,
Now silent sits where frost hath kissed the ground,
The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and bright,
Now murmurs low where winter's hush is found.

Yet mark the rime that decks the barren bough,
And paints the field with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the rose, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet time's harsh hand doth bear them all away.
