**Sonnet of the Seasons' Dance**

When April's breath doth wake the slumbering rose,
And summer's gold yields to autumn's pale hue,
What subtle hand unseen the bloom bestows,
Then steals the light of beauty bright and true?

The lily, once in ivory pride arrayed,
Now bows her head where winter's frost doth creep,
The skylark's song in hollow echoes fades,
And silence wraps the woods in frozen sleep.

Yet mark how silver threads the black bough's lace,
And paints the mead with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that walks in heaven's starry space,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet nature's hand doth bid them rise alway.
