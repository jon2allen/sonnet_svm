**Sonnet of the Ebbing Tide**

When golden hours like leaves before the wind
Do scatter wide and fade into the air,
What power unseen doth bid the fairest end,
And turn to dust the joys that once were fair?

The violet, once in purple pride arrayed,
Now droops her head where winter's breath doth creep,
The lark that soared in morning's golden shade
Now silent lies where frost hath sealed her sleep.

Yet mark how silver threads the black bough's lace,
And paints the mead with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens' grace,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet nature's hand doth weave them to decay.
