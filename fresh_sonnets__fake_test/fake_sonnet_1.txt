**Sonnet of the Fleeting Year**

O fleeting hour, that steals with silent tread,
And bears the bloom from beauty’s blushing cheek,
While golden locks to silver threads are wed,
And time’s harsh hand doth youth’s sweet promise break.

The rose, once proud in crimson robe arrayed,
Now droops her head where frost hath kissed her bed,
And summer’s song in winter’s sighs doth fade,
As leaves descend where once the green was spread.

Yet in this fall, a quiet grace is born—
The naked bough, the pale and pensive sky,
The brook that hums a dirge for days outworn,
The star that gleams where twilight’s shadows lie.

So too shall pass the joys that now we keep,
But nature’s hand shall wake them from their sleep.
