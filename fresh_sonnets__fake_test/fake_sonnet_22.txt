**Sonnet of the Fleeting Light**

When golden hours like leaves before the blast
Do scatter wide and fade into the air,
What power unseen doth bid the fairest last,
And turn to dust the joys that once were fair?

The rose that bloomed in crimson, proud array,
Now droops her head where winter's breath doth creep,
The nightingale that sang the livelong day
Now silent lies where frost hath sealed her sleep.

Yet lo! the rime that decks the barren bough,
And paints the mead with hues of silver bright,
The moon that walks in solitary grace,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting breath,
Yet nature's hand doth guard them from dark death.
