**Sonnet of Time's Gentle Weaving**

When April's breath doth wake the sleeping rose,
And summer's gold gives way to autumn's gray,
What hidden weaver shapes each fleeting pose,
Then steals the light of beauty's bright array?

The violet, once in purple pride displayed,
Now bows her head where winter's chill doth creep,
The skylark's song in hollow echoes fades,
And silence wraps the woods in frozen sleep.

Yet lo! the frost that decks the barren bough,
And paints the mead with silver, pale and bright,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet time's own loom doth weave them back to flower.
