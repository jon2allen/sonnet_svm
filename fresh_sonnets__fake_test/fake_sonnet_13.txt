**Sonnet of the Shifting Year**

When April's blush hath left the blushing rose,
And summer's gold is turned to ashen gray,
What power unseen doth bid the blossom close,
And steal the light of youth's resplendent day?

The nightingale, whose song did once enthrall,
Now silent sits where autumn's chill doth creep,
The brook that danced in silver, bright, and tall,
Now whispers low where withered branches weep.

Yet lo! the frost that gilds the leafless tree,
And paints the field with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that rules the star-strewn canopy,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet nature's hand doth guard their fading power.
