**Sonnet of the Vanishing Hour**

When golden noon doth yield to evening's shade,
And time's swift hand doth pluck the trembling rose,
What power unseen doth bid the blossom fade,
And steal the hue where youth's bright glory glows?

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and bright,
Now murmurs low beneath the winter's breath,
The lark that soared in morning's golden light
Lies still and cold, a prey to silent death.

Yet mark how frost doth deck the leafless tree,
And paints the field with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that rules the star-strewn canopy,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting day,
Yet nature's hand doth guard their fading ray.
