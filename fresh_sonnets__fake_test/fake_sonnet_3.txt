**Sonnet of the Fleeting Light**

O cruelest thief, that dost in silence creep,
And pluck the rose ere yet her prime be spent,
What art thou, Time, that mak’st the strong to weep,
And steal’st the fire where youth’s bright embers blent?

The brook that laughed in silver, clear, and bright,
Now murmurs low beneath the winter’s breath,
The lark that soared in morning’s golden light
Lies still and cold, a prey to silent death.

Yet mark how frost doth lace the withered bough,
And paint the field with hues of ghostly white,
The moon, pale queen of heaven’s starry brow,
Doth rule the night with melancholy light.

So fades the bloom that once did grace the earth,
Yet in its fall, doth teach the soul its worth.
