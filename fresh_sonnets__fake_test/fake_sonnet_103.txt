**Sonnet of Nature's Endless Dance**

When golden dawn doth kiss the waking earth,
And noon's bright fire yields to twilight's grace,
What subtle power gives beauty fleeting birth,
Then steals the light from each fair form's embrace?

The rose that bloomed in crimson, proud display,
Now droops her head where winter's breath doth creep,
The nightingale that sang the livelong day
Now silent sits where frost hath sealed her sleep.

Yet mark how silver decks the barren bough,
And paints the mead with hues of ghostly white,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet nature's dance doth bid them rise anew.
