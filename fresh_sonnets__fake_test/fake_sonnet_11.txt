**Sonnet of the Vanishing Hour**

When dawn's first blush doth yield to noon's bright fire,
And noon to eve's more melancholy hue,
What subtle thief doth steal each sweet desire,
And leave the world in shadows cold and blue?

The rose that bloomed in crimson, proud array,
Now droops her head where winter's breath hath blown,
The nightingale that sang the livelong day
Now silent sits on branches bare and lone.

Yet mark how frost doth gild the withered bough,
And paint the mead with silver, pale and bright,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet nature's hand doth guard their fading power.
