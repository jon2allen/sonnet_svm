**Sonnet of the Seasons' Wheel**

When golden dawn doth kiss the waking earth,
And noon's bright fire yields to twilight's hue,
What subtle hand unseen gives beauty birth,
Then steals the light that once so brightly grew?

The rose that bloomed in crimson, proud display,
Now droops her head where winter's breath hath blown,
The lark that sang the livelong summer's day
Now silent sits on branches bare and lone.

Yet mark how frost doth deck the withered bough,
And paint the mead with silver, pale and bright,
The moon that walks the star-strewn heavens now,
And stars that gem the sable robe of night.

So fades the bloom, so fleets the fleeting hour,
Yet time's great wheel doth guard their fading power.
